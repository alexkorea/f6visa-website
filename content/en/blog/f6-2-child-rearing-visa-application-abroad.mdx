# Coming to Korea to Raise Your Korean Child: Child-Rearing (F-6-2) Visa Documents and the 90 Days After Entry

A father or mother who wants to enter Korea to raise a minor child after the marriage ended applies for a Child-Rearing (F-6-2) visa at a Korean diplomatic mission abroad. The visa is issued as a single-entry visa with a stay of 90 days or less (multiple-entry for US nationals), and alien registration and extension of stay must be done within 90 days of entry. Marriage Migrant (F-6) is not eligible for a visa issuance confirmation, so plan to apply at the mission.

## Key Summary

- Eligible: a father or mother who raises, or intends to raise, in Korea a minor child born of a marriage (including a de facto marriage) with a Korean national after the marriage ended
- Visa: single-entry visa with a stay of 90 days or less (multiple-entry for US nationals)
- Attachments: visa application form, passport, photo, fee; proof of family (parent-child) relationship; proof of child-rearing; criminal record certificate and health certificate
- Not eligible for a visa issuance confirmation
- Alien registration and extension within 90 days of entry; registering after 90 days is treated as a violation

## 1. Who applies for F-6-2 from abroad?

F-6-2 is the status for a father or mother who raises, or intends to raise, in Korea a minor child born of a marriage (including a de facto marriage) with a Korean national after the marriage ended. Because it covers not only those already raising the child in Korea but also those who intend to, a parent abroad who wants to enter Korea to raise the child considers this visa.

## 2. What kind of visa is issued?

A person entering for Child-Rearing (F-6-2) receives a single-entry visa with a stay of 90 days or less. US nationals receive a multiple-entry visa. When the visa is issued, you are told to complete alien registration and extension of stay within 90 days of entry.

## 3. Which documents are attached?

- Visa application form, passport, 1 standard photo, fee
- Proof of family (parent-child) relationship: the child’s basic certificate, birth certificate, DNA test confirmation, or other documents confirming the family relationship
- If the child was born in a de facto marriage, documents proving that fact
- Proof of child-rearing: a judgment containing custody terms, a child-rearing confirmation from a Korean relative of the child within the 5th degree (including the father or mother), or similar documents
- Criminal record certificate and health certificate

The head of the mission may add or omit some documents or request additional ones where especially necessary.

## 4. Can I apply with a visa issuance confirmation instead?

Marriage Migrant (F-6) is not eligible for a visa issuance confirmation. So instead of obtaining a confirmation in Korea and sending it, the applicant applies for the visa directly at the mission.

## 5. What must I do after entering?

Within 90 days of entering on the Child-Rearing (F-6-2, 90 days) visa, apply for extension of stay and alien registration at the immigration office for your address. The stay granted is stated as 1 year from the date of entry; whether 2 years applies after completing the early adaptation program needs to be confirmed with the immigration office case by case. You submit the application form, passport, 1 standard photo and fee, along with the child’s basic certificate and family relation certificate if the child is a Korean national, a foreigner occupation report and proof of residence. Registering more than 90 days after entry is treated as a violation, so keep to the deadline.

## 6. Common problems

- The judgment contains no custody terms, so other child-rearing evidence must be prepared
- The child was born in a de facto marriage, but no document proving it is ready
- Seeing only the 90-day stay on the visa and missing the alien registration deadline
- Delays in issuing the criminal record or health certificate in the home country push back the schedule
