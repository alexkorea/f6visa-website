# Applying Again for an F-6-1 Visa After a Previous Marriage Migrant (F-6) Stay in Korea — The Simplified Document List for the Same Spouse With No Break in the Marriage

If the foreign spouse once lived in Korea with marriage migrant (F-6) status and the marriage to the same Korean spouse has continued without interruption, the couple can use the document list for "cases where the applicant previously stayed in Korea with marriage migrant (F-6) status" when applying again for a spouse of a Korean national (F-6-1) visa. The list is much shorter than the general one, but it does not apply if the spouse has changed or if the marriage was ever interrupted, even with the same spouse.

This guide is for couples where the foreign spouse lived in Korea on F-6 before, left Korea, and now wants to return with the same spouse.

## Key Summary

- Who: the foreign spouse previously stayed in Korea with marriage migrant (F-6) status (long or short term)
- Excluded: the spouse has changed, or the marriage was interrupted at some point even with the same spouse
- Simplified list: basic documents, invitation letter and letter of surety, Korean spouse's certificates, foreign spouse's documents, housing documents, and the common required relationship evidence
- Not on the list: marriage background statement, income documents, communication (Korean language) documents, and documents tied to how the couple met
- The simplified list is the same for international marriage guidance program countries (China, Vietnam, the Philippines, Cambodia, Mongolia, Uzbekistan, Thailand) and all other countries
- Visa issued: single-entry visa with a period of stay of 90 days or less; alien registration and extension within 90 days of entry

## 1. Who can use this simplified list?

The test is whether "the foreign spouse previously stayed in Korea with marriage migrant (F-6) status." The same condition appears in the exemptions for the income requirement, the communication requirement, and the documents on how the couple met, and the communication and how-you-met exemptions state that it does not matter whether the stay was long or short term.

Every one of these rules carries the same proviso: it does not apply if the spouse has changed or if the marriage was interrupted, even with the same spouse. The exemption wording for marriage broker documents likewise refers to a stay "with the same Korean national without any past interruption of the marriage." In short, the marriage to the same Korean spouse must have continued from the start until now.

## 2. The full simplified document list

| Category | Documents |
|---|---|
| Basic documents | Application form, passport photo, applicant's (foreign spouse's) original passport and 1 copy, application fee |
| Documents to fill in | Invitation letter for a foreign spouse, letter of surety |
| Korean spouse | 1 passport copy, 1 basic certificate (detailed), 1 marriage relation certificate (detailed), 1 family relation certificate (detailed), 1 resident registration copy (Korean original) |
| Foreign spouse | 1 home-country marriage certificate (only when needed), 1 tuberculosis certificate (only for high-risk TB countries), other documents that apply specifically to the country (e.g. the Philippine CFO training certificate, original and 1 copy) |
| Housing | Owned home: property register / rented home: property register and 1 copy of the lease |
| Relationship evidence | Common required documents |

The common required relationship evidence is material that shows how the relationship developed and that the marriage is genuine: dating photos, family photos, SNS conversations and so on, arranged as you like on A4 paper, within 5 pages. The home-country marriage certificate is optional and requested only when needed, for example to check for bigamy in the home country.

## 3. What is left out compared with the general list?

Compared with the general list, the simplified list does not include:

- The foreign spouse's marriage background statement
- The Korean spouse's income documents (income amount certificate, credit information report, etc.)
- The foreign spouse's communication documents (Korean language documents, etc.)
- Documents tied to how the couple met (copy of the marriage broker's registration, copy of the surety insurance policy, copy of the contract; copy of the introducer's ID)

The international marriage guidance program certificate, the Korean spouse's health certificate, and the foreign spouse's criminal record certificate and health certificate, which appear on the general list for program countries, are also not written on the simplified list. However, documents may be added or removed during intake and review, so what can actually be left out follows the guidance of the embassy or consulate where you apply and needs to be confirmed case by case.

## 4. How do international marriage guidance program countries differ from other countries?

The guidance program applies to people who invite, for the purpose of married cohabitation, a national of a specific country designated by the Minister of Justice; those countries are China, Vietnam, the Philippines, Cambodia, Mongolia, Uzbekistan and Thailand. The document lists are also split into "program countries" and "other countries," and only the general list for program countries has an item for the international marriage guidance program certificate.

The list for "cases where the applicant previously stayed in Korea with marriage migrant (F-6) status," however, consists of the same documents in both country groups and carries the same proviso (not applicable if the spouse changed or the marriage was interrupted). What differs by country group is the general list, and if you do not meet the simplified conditions you follow the general list for your country group.

## 5. If the spouse has changed or the marriage was interrupted

In that case the simplified list does not apply, and the past F-6 stay cannot be used as grounds for the income, communication or how-you-met exemptions. For example, someone who once stayed on F-6 through a marriage to a different Korean and now applies with a new spouse, or someone who divorced and then remarried the same person, must prepare the full general list.

If the couple has a child born between them, there is a separate list for "cases where the couple has a child born between them," but that list includes the marriage background statement and the child's family relation certificate (detailed). Which list you can use depends on your marriage history and needs to be confirmed case by case.

## 6. Points to watch when preparing documents

- Validity: unless otherwise specified, documents such as the invitation letter and marriage relation certificate must be within 3 months of the date written or issued.
- Korean spouse's certificates: the basic, marriage relation and family relation certificates must be the "detailed" versions, and the resident registration copy must be the Korean original.
- Matching address: the address submitted for the housing requirement must match the address on the resident registration copy.
- Passport: the remarks column of the general list says the applicant's original passport must have at least 6 months of remaining validity.
- Additional documents: additional documents beyond those listed may be requested during individual review.
- After entry: the F-6-1 visa is issued as a single-entry visa with a period of stay of 90 days or less (multiple-entry for US nationals), and alien registration and extension of stay must be done within 90 days of entry.

## 7. Common problems

- Treating a divorce and remarriage to the same person as "the same spouse" and preparing the simplified list
- Leaving out income and Korean language documents because of a past F-6 stay even though the spouse has changed
- Forgetting the tuberculosis certificate when the foreign spouse is from a high-risk TB country
- Obtaining certificates too early so that more than 3 months have passed since issue at the time of application

## Frequently Asked Questions

### My previous F-6 stay was short. Can I still use the simplified list?

The exemptions for the communication requirement and how-you-met documents state that it does not matter whether the stay was long or short term. The spouse must be the same and the marriage must never have been interrupted.

### We divorced and then remarried each other. Does this apply?

The rule says it does not apply if the marriage was interrupted, even with the same spouse, so you are not eligible for the simplified list. You need to prepare the general list.

### Do I not need the marriage background statement?

The documents-to-fill-in row of the simplified list names only the invitation letter for a foreign spouse and the letter of surety; the marriage background statement is not listed. The embassy or consulate may still request it during review.

### Is it the same list when changing to F-6-1 status inside Korea?

The documents for a change of status to spouse of a Korean national (F-6-1) follow those for the F-6-1 visa, and some documents may be added or removed during intake and review. Whether a change of status is possible at all depends on your current status and needs to be confirmed case by case.
