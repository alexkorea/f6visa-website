# Is the Child of a Korean and a Foreign Spouse a Korean National? Birth Registration Deadline, Who Files, When to Choose a Nationality and the Effect on the Marriage Migrant's Stay

Yes. If either the father or the mother is a Korean national at the time of birth, the child acquires Korean nationality at the moment of birth. No separate nationality application is needed, but the birth must be reported within 1 month so that it is recorded in the family relation register, and if the child also holds the foreign parent's nationality, one nationality must be chosen by a set time.

This guide is for marriage migrants who have had, or are expecting, a child with a Korean spouse, and for their families. The child's nationality and birth report are also linked to the documents for the foreign parent's stay extension.

## Key Summary

- If the father or mother is a Korean national at the time of birth, the child acquires Korean nationality at birth (it does not matter which parent is Korean)
- If the father died before the birth, the same applies if he was a Korean national at the time of death
- The birth must be reported within 1 month of birth; for a child born in wedlock, the father or mother files the report
- The report states the foreign parent's name, date of birth, nationality and alien registration number and, if the child has dual nationality, that fact and the foreign nationality
- Failing to report within the period without a justifiable reason can lead to an administrative fine of up to 50,000 won
- A child who became a dual national before turning 20 must choose one nationality before turning 22 (by renouncing the foreign nationality or pledging not to exercise it, and then filing a report)
- A spouse of a Korean national (F-6-1) raising a Korean child can be granted up to 3 years at extension, and the extension documents include the child's family relation certificate

## 1. Does the child become a Korean national at birth?

The Nationality Act provides that a person whose father or mother is a Korean national at the time of birth acquires Korean nationality at birth. It does not matter whether the Korean parent is the father or the mother. If the father died before the child was born, the same applies if he was a Korean national at the time of his death.

| Situation | Child's Korean nationality |
|---|---|
| Korean father, foreign mother | Acquired at birth |
| Korean mother, foreign father | Acquired at birth |
| Korean father died before the birth | Acquired if the father was Korean at the time of death |

Many countries also grant nationality by descent, so the child may hold both Korean and a foreign nationality. Whether and how the foreign nationality is acquired depends on that country's law and needs to be checked case by case.

## 2. When, by whom and where is the birth reported?

A birth must be reported within 1 month of birth. For a child born in wedlock, the father or mother files the report. For a child born out of wedlock, the mother files, and if the person who must report cannot do so, a relative living with the child, then the doctor, midwife or other person who assisted the delivery, files in that order.

| Item | Details |
|---|---|
| Deadline | Within 1 month of birth |
| Who files | Born in wedlock: father or mother / born out of wedlock: mother |
| If that person cannot file | A relative living together → doctor, midwife or other person who assisted the delivery, in that order |
| Where | The registration base of the person concerned, the filer's address or current location, or the place of birth |
| Attachment | A birth certificate prepared by a doctor or midwife (or another document set out in the law if there is none) |

The foreign parent can file the report. The report contains the parents' details; if the father or mother is a foreigner, their name, date of birth, nationality and alien registration number are entered. If the child is a dual national, that fact and the foreign nationality acquired must also be entered. The child's name must be written in Hangul or commonly used Chinese characters.

If the child was born abroad, the Korean parent can file with the head of the Korean diplomatic mission with jurisdiction over that area.

## 3. What happens if the birth report is late?

A person obliged to report who fails to do so within the period without a justifiable reason is subject to an administrative fine of up to 50,000 won. If the head of the city, town or township office sets a period and demands the report, and it is still not filed within that period without a justifiable reason, the fine can be up to 100,000 won.

For a child born at a medical institution in Korea, the head of the institution submits the birth information to the Health Insurance Review and Assessment Service within 14 days of birth, and it is passed on to the head of the city, town or township office. If no report is filed after the reporting period, that office demands that the person obliged to report file within 7 days, and if it is still not filed, records the birth ex officio with the permission of the supervising court. The birth will be registered even if late, but in the meantime a family relation certificate cannot be issued, which can delay the stay documents.

## 4. If the child has dual nationality, by when must a nationality be chosen?

A person who holds both Korean and a foreign nationality from birth is treated only as a Korean national when Korean laws are applied, and must choose one nationality by the following time.

| Case | When to choose |
|---|---|
| Became a dual national before turning 20 (including at birth) | Before turning 22 |
| Became a dual national after turning 20 | Within 2 years from then |
| Enlisted in the military service preparation category | Within 3 months of enlistment, or within 2 years of a case set out in the law, such as completing service |

To choose Korean nationality within the period, the person either renounces the foreign nationality or pledges not to exercise the foreign nationality in Korea, and reports the choice of Korean nationality. After the period, Korean nationality can be chosen only if the foreign nationality has been renounced. A person enlisted in the military service preparation category who wishes to choose Korean nationality can do so even before completing service.

If no nationality is chosen within the period, the Minister of Justice orders the person to choose one within 1 year, and a person who does not follow the order loses Korean nationality when that period ends. Also, if it is recognized that the mother was staying abroad at the time of birth for the purpose of having the child acquire a foreign nationality, Korean nationality can be chosen only after renouncing the foreign nationality.

## 5. How does a Korean child affect the foreign parent's stay?

When a spouse of a Korean national (F-6-1) extends the stay, the permitted period is in principle up to 1 year, but if the spouse is raising a child born with the Korean spouse, it can be granted for up to 3 years. The extension documents include the child's family relation certificate if the couple has a child, so the birth report must be done first.

A father or mother raising in Korea a minor child born from a marriage (including a de facto marriage) with a Korean national can stay with child-rearing (F-6-2) status, and if the child is Korean, the child's basic certificate and family relation certificate are submitted. A child-rearing (F-6-2) extension is for up to 3 years and requires proof of continued child-rearing, such as school fee receipts or the child's hospital receipts.

| Situation | Permitted period | Child-related documents |
|---|---|---|
| Spouse of a Korean national (F-6-1) extension, raising a child | Up to 3 years | Child's family relation certificate |
| Child-rearing (F-6-2) extension | Up to 3 years | Child's basic certificate and family relation certificate, proof of child-rearing |

## 6. Common problems

- Registering the birth only in the foreign parent's country and delaying the Korean birth report past 1 month
- The Korean spouse being too busy to report, without knowing that the foreign parent can also file
- Not entering the child's dual nationality and foreign nationality on the report
- Missing documents at extension because the child's family relation certificate has not yet been issued
- Missing the dual-national child's choice period, leaving renunciation of the foreign nationality as the only option

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Does the child get Korean nationality if only the mother is Korean?

Yes. A child whose father or mother is a Korean national at the time of birth acquires Korean nationality at birth, so the same applies if only the mother is Korean.

### Can the foreign parent report the birth alone?

The birth of a child born in wedlock is reported by the father or mother, so the foreign parent can also file. The report contains the foreign parent's name, date of birth, nationality and alien registration number.

### What happens if the birth report deadline is missed?

Failing to report within 1 month without a justifiable reason is subject to a fine of up to 50,000 won, and failing to report within the period of a formal demand is subject to a fine of up to 100,000 won.

### By when must a dual-national child decide on a nationality?

A person who became a dual national before turning 20 must choose one nationality before turning 22. There is a separate deadline for those enlisted in the military service preparation category.

### Does having a child lengthen the marriage migrant's stay?

A spouse of a Korean national (F-6-1) raising a child born with the Korean spouse can be granted up to 3 years at extension. The actual period is decided in the review.
