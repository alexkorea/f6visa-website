# F-6 Marriage Visa Health Certificate and TB Test: What You Need

For an F-6-1 marriage visa, the foreign spouse's health certificate must be issued within 6 months before the visa application date, and it must include a tuberculosis (TB) test item in addition to the standard checkup items. The Korean spouse also submits a certificate in a set format, but if the couple is exempt from the International Marriage Guidance Program, the health certificate is waived and only a TB-related certificate for the foreign spouse may remain.

## Key Summary

- Foreign spouse: issued within 6 months of the application date, with hospital name, address, contact and the doctor's signature, typed by computer
- A general checkup without a TB test item is unlikely to be accepted
- Korean spouse: certificate in the civil-service recruitment checkup format, within 6 months
- If exempt from the guidance program, the health certificate is waived but a TB-related certificate is still submitted

## 1. Why does a marriage visa need a health certificate?

The manual lists health certificates for both the Korean and the foreign spouse among the basic documents when inviting a spouse from a country covered by the International Marriage Guidance Program. They are submitted together with the invitation letter and family relation records.

## 2. What format must the foreign spouse's certificate follow?

- Issued within 6 months before the visa application date
- Shows the hospital name, address, contact number and the attending doctor's signature
- Prepared on a computer
- Includes a tuberculosis (TB) test item beyond the checkup items shown in the manual

Handwritten forms or certificates missing the hospital details or signature may trigger a request for supplements, so tell the clinic the requirements before the exam.

## 3. What does the Korean spouse submit?

A certificate issued by a hospital-level medical institution, a public health center, or a checkup institution under the civil-service recruitment medical examination rules, in the civil-service recruitment checkup form, also issued within 6 months before the application date.

## 4. Are there exemptions?

Couples exempt from the guidance program (for example, those who can prove a courtship during long-term residence, or cases needing humanitarian consideration such as pregnancy or childbirth) do not submit the health certificates. The foreign spouse must still submit a TB-related certificate. If pregnancy makes TB diagnosis difficult, the manual says to inquire separately.

## 5. Which spouses need the TB certificate?

The simplified document lists show "TB-related certificate (high-risk countries only)". The manual includes a list of 35 high-TB-burden countries as of 1 April 2020, including China, Vietnam, the Philippines, Thailand, Cambodia, Uzbekistan and Mongolia. Which document set applies differs by case, so confirm before filing.

## 6. Common problems

- Counting the 6 months from the exam date instead of the visa application date, so the certificate expires
- Getting a general checkup without the TB test item
- Missing hospital contact details or doctor's signature
- Replacing the Korean spouse's form with an ordinary checkup result

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How long is an F-6 health certificate valid?

It must be issued within 6 months before the visa application date. If filing is delayed after the exam, you may need a new one.

### Is the TB test mandatory?

The foreign spouse's certificate must include a TB test item. Even for exempt couples, a TB-related certificate is still to be submitted.

### Does the Korean spouse also need a medical exam?

For spouses from guidance-program countries, the Korean spouse's health certificate is part of the basic documents; exempt couples do not submit it.

### What if I am pregnant and cannot take the TB test?

The manual says to inquire separately when pregnancy makes TB diagnosis difficult, so check with the immigration office or Korean mission before filing.

### Where do we get the certificates?

The foreign spouse gets it at a hospital; the Korean spouse at a hospital-level institution or public health center. Follow HiKorea and the Korean mission's notice for the exact form.
