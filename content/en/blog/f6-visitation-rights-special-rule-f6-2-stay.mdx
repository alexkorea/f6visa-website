# Seeing Your Child After Divorce: The Visitation-Rights Special Rule for Stay (F-6-2)

A person staying as the spouse of a Korean national (F-6-1) whose marriage ended, and who holds visitation rights to a child born to the Korean spouse, can receive F-6-2 stay under a special rule. Visitation rights must not have been restricted or excluded by a family court decision or the like, and there must be continuing contact with the child; if either is missing the stay is not permitted, and the grant is for a range of 1 year as F-6-2.

## Key Summary

- Eligible: F-6-1 holders whose marriage ended and who hold visitation rights to a child born to the Korean spouse
- Criteria (all required, field investigation if needed): visitation not restricted or excluded; continuing contact with the child
- Stay denied if visitation is restricted or excluded or there is no contact
- Grant: within a range of 1 year as F-6-2

## 1. What is the visitation-rights special rule?

It opens a stay route for a person who no longer raises the child directly after the marriage ended but holds visitation rights to the child born to the Korean spouse.

## 2. Who is covered?

A person who stayed as the spouse of a Korean national (F-6-1) whose marriage ended and who holds visitation rights to a child born to the Korean spouse.

## 3. What are the criteria?

Both criteria below must be met, with a field investigation if needed.
- Visitation rights have not been restricted or excluded by a family court decision or the like
- There is continuing contact with the child
If visitation is restricted or excluded, or there is no contact with the child, the stay is not permitted.

## 4. Which documents are submitted?

- Application form
- Child’s basic certificate and family relation certificate if the child is a Korean national
- Divorce judgment, etc. (to check whether visitation is restricted; may be omitted for divorce by agreement)
- Documents such as photos proving continuing contact with the child
- Foreigner occupation report
- Other documents the office considers necessary
The divorce judgment is collected only for the first extension after the marriage ended and omitted afterward.

## 5. How long is the stay granted?

Within a range of 1 year as F-6-2.

## 6. Common problems

- Only a few old photos, making “continuing” contact hard to show
- Court decision documents not checked, so lack of restriction cannot be shown
- Divorce by agreement leaving no judgment and uncertainty about how to explain it

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can I stay with only visitation rights?

Under the manual’s special rule, if visitation is not restricted or excluded and there is continuing contact with the child, you may be eligible for F-6-2 stay.

### When is it denied?

If visitation rights are restricted or excluded, or there is no contact with the child.

### How is contact proven?

With documents such as photos that prove continuing contact with the child.

### What if my divorce was by agreement and there is no judgment?

The manual says the divorce judgment may be omitted for a divorce by agreement.

### How long is the stay granted?

Within a range of 1 year as F-6-2.
