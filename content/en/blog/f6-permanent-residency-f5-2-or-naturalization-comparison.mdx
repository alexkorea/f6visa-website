# Permanent Residency (F-5-2) or Naturalization for Marriage Migrants? Differences in Requirements, Nationality, Re-entry and Voting, and Naturalizing After Permanent Residency

The two paths start from similar requirements but lead to different results. Permanent residency (F-5-2) is a status of stay that lets you live in Korea without a time limit while remaining a foreign national; naturalization means acquiring Korean nationality and becoming a Korean citizen. A marriage migrant who has kept the marriage with a Korean spouse and lived in Korea for 2 years or more can consider both, and holding permanent residency does not stop you from applying for naturalization later.

This guide compares the two for foreign spouses in Marriage Migrant (F-6) status, and their Korean spouses, who are thinking about the next step. Detailed requirements of each system are covered in separate guides listed under Related Guides.

## Key Summary

- Permanent residency (F-5-2): a person who maintains a normal marital life with a Korean spouse and has stayed in Korea continuously for 2 years or more in Marriage Migrant (F-6) status
- Simplified naturalization (marriage maintained): a person who has had a continuous address in Korea for 2 years or more while married, or for 1 year or more while married after 3 years have passed since the marriage
- Both look at conduct, ability to support oneself and basic competency, but the standards and ways of proof differ
- Permanent residency is a status of stay for a foreigner, so your original nationality stays as it is; after naturalization you must, within 1 year of acquiring Korean nationality, renounce the foreign nationality or pledge not to exercise it
- A permanent resident who re-enters within 2 years of departure is exempt from a re-entry permit, and the permanent residence card is reissued every 10 years
- 3 years after acquiring permanent residency you get the right to vote in local elections; as a naturalized citizen aged 18 or over you can vote for the President and the National Assembly
- You can apply for simplified naturalization as the spouse of a Korean national even while holding permanent residency

## 1. What is the fundamental difference?

Permanent residency is a status of stay that allows a person to reside permanently in Korea, and a foreigner holding it is not restricted in the scope of activities or period of stay. The nationality does not change and the person remains a foreigner.

Naturalization is how a foreigner who has never held Korean nationality acquires it with naturalization permission from the Minister of Justice. A person granted permission acquires nationality when taking the oath of nationality before the Minister of Justice and receiving the naturalization certificate.

| Item | Permanent residency (F-5-2) | Simplified naturalization (spouse of a Korean) |
|---|---|---|
| Result | Foreigner with permanent residency | Korean national |
| Governing law | Immigration Act | Nationality Act |
| When it takes effect | Permission to change status of stay | When receiving the naturalization certificate after the oath |
| Original nationality | Kept | Renounce within 1 year or pledge not to exercise it |

## 2. How do the requirements differ? Period of stay, livelihood, basic competency

The period is similar. Permanent residency (F-5-2) requires 2 years or more of continuous stay in Korea in Marriage Migrant (F-6) status. Simplified naturalization requires a continuous address in Korea for 2 years or more while married, or for 1 year or more while married after 3 years have passed since the marriage. Naturalization also requires being an adult under the Civil Act.

| Requirement | Permanent residency (F-5-2) | Simplified naturalization (spouse of a Korean) |
|---|---|---|
| Ability to support oneself | Income of the applicant or family sharing a livelihood at or above the previous year's per capita gross national income (GNI), or household assets at or above the median net assets | One of: financial assets of 30 million won or more, real estate or a lease deposit of 30 million won or more, or a certificate of employment or of expected employment |
| Basic competency | Completion of the Social Integration Program or a comprehensive evaluation score of 60 or more | Comprehensive evaluation and interview (Social Integration Program completers are exempt from the evaluation) |
| Conduct | Good conduct, such as obeying the law | Good conduct, such as obeying the law |

For permanent residency (F-5-2), the income standard and the asset standard cannot be added together. Family sharing a livelihood means the spouse, the applicant's lineal family and the spouse's lineal family who have lived with the applicant continuously for 1 year or more. Under a Ministry of Justice notice, the livelihood or basic competency requirement may be eased or waived in some cases, which needs to be confirmed case by case.

## 3. How is basic competency reviewed?

For permanent residency (F-5-2), basic competency is proven with a Social Integration Program completion certificate (Korean Immigration Permanent Residency Aptitude Course or Korean Immigration Naturalization Aptitude Course), or a pass certificate of the KIPRAT or KINAT showing a comprehensive evaluation score of 60 or more.

For naturalization, a comprehensive evaluation and an interview are conducted. A person who completed the Social Integration Program, or who scored 60 or more in the comprehensive evaluation within the last 3 years before the application date, may be exempted from the evaluation, and a Social Integration Program completer who scored 60 or more in the comprehensive evaluation may also be exempted from the interview. The interview checks Korean language ability, the attitude of a Korean national and belief in the liberal democratic basic order, among other things.

A naturalization applicant must sit the comprehensive evaluation within 1 year of the application date. Not sitting it within that period, scoring below 60, or receiving an unsuitable rating in the interview means naturalization will not be permitted.

## 4. What happens to my original nationality?

Permanent residency is a status of stay given to a foreigner, so your original nationality does not change.

A person who acquired Korean nationality by naturalization and still holds a foreign nationality must renounce it within 1 year of acquiring Korean nationality. However, a person naturalized as the spouse of a Korean national under the 2-year address requirement while married, or the 3-year marriage and 1-year address requirement, may instead pledge to the Minister of Justice not to exercise the foreign nationality in Korea. If neither is done within 1 year, Korean nationality is lost when that period ends.

A person who makes the pledge and holds both nationalities is treated only as a Korean national under Korean law. Whether your home country allows dual nationality depends on its own law and needs to be checked separately. Simplified naturalization after the marriage has ended, such as by the spouse's death or divorce, is not among the grounds eligible for the pledge.

## 5. How do re-entry and stability of status differ?

| Item | Permanent residency (F-5-2) | After naturalization (Korean national) |
|---|---|---|
| Re-entry | No re-entry permit needed if re-entering within 2 years of departure | Travels as a Korean national |
| Registration card | Permanent residence card (valid 10 years, reissue before expiry) | Not applicable (national) |
| When status is lost | Limited to the grounds for cancelling permanent residency set by law | Grounds for loss of nationality under the Nationality Act (such as not renouncing or pledging within 1 year) |

Not reissuing the permanent residence card makes you subject to a fine for negligence. Permanent residency can be cancelled only in limited cases, such as obtaining it by false or other unlawful means, a final sentence of imprisonment of 2 years or more for certain crimes, sentences within the last 5 years totalling 3 years or more, or acts against the national interest. A permanent resident is not deported unless falling under exceptions set by law, such as crimes of insurrection or foreign aggression.

For reference, the re-entry permit exemption for Marriage Migrant (F-6) status is 1 year from departure.

## 6. How do voting rights differ?

A foreigner aged 18 or over for whom 3 years have passed since acquiring permanent residency and who is listed in the foreigner registration ledger of the local government has the right to vote for local council members and the head of that local government. The right to vote for the President and the National Assembly belongs to nationals aged 18 or over, so it comes only with naturalization.

## 7. Can I still naturalize after getting permanent residency?

Yes. General naturalization requires an address in Korea for 5 years or more and a status of stay allowing permanent residence, but a foreigner whose spouse is a Korean national can apply for simplified naturalization without these two requirements once the address requirement while married is met. You can get permanent residency (F-5-2) first to stabilize your stay and prepare naturalization later, or apply for naturalization directly without permanent residency. Either way, you must meet the naturalization requirements, such as ability to support oneself and basic competency, at the naturalization stage.

Points to weigh when choosing:

- Whether you need to keep your original nationality (property, family, whether your home country's law allows dual nationality)
- Whether you need the status of a national, such as voting for the President and the National Assembly
- Whether you often stay in your home country for long periods, so the re-entry exemption period matters
- Which livelihood standard your current income and assets meet, and whether you have completed the Social Integration Program

## 8. Common problems

- Thinking that once you get permanent residency you can no longer naturalize
- Assuming naturalization always means giving up your original nationality, or the opposite, missing the 1-year deadline for the pledge
- Treating the livelihood standard for permanent residency (F-5-2) and the livelihood documents for naturalization as the same thing
- Assuming that completing the Social Integration Program automatically exempts you from the naturalization interview
- Missing the 10-year reissue deadline for the permanent residence card

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Are the period-of-stay requirements for F-5-2 and simplified naturalization the same?

They are similar. F-5-2 requires 2 years or more of continuous stay in Marriage Migrant (F-6) status; simplified naturalization requires a continuous address in Korea for 2 years or more while married, or for 1 year or more while married after 3 years of marriage.

### Do I have to give up my original nationality if I naturalize?

If you naturalized as the spouse of a Korean national while the marriage was maintained, you may, within 1 year, pledge not to exercise the foreign nationality in Korea instead of renouncing it. Whether your home country allows dual nationality needs to be checked separately.

### Can I not apply for naturalization if I have permanent residency?

You can. Even while holding permanent residency, you can apply for simplified naturalization as the spouse of a Korean national, and the naturalization requirements are reviewed at that time.

### Can permanent residents vote?

A person aged 18 or over for whom 3 years have passed since acquiring permanent residency and who is listed in the local government's foreigner registration ledger can vote in local elections. Only nationals can vote for the President and the National Assembly.

### How soon must a permanent resident return to be exempt from a re-entry permit?

Re-entering within 2 years of departure is exempt from a re-entry permit. For Marriage Migrant (F-6) status it is 1 year.
