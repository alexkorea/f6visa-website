# Can a Marriage Migrant Still Apply for Simplified Naturalization After the Korean Spouse Dies, After a Divorce, or While Raising a Child? Requirements and Documents

Yes. The Nationality Act allows simplified naturalization for a person who, while married to a Korean national and domiciled in Korea, could no longer continue a normal married life because of the spouse's death or disappearance or another reason for which the person is not responsible, provided the person has completed the remaining part of the required period and the Minister of Justice finds it appropriate. A person who is raising, or must raise, a minor child born of that marriage can also be naturalized in the same way if the required period has been completed.

This guide is for marriage migrants who stay as a person whose marriage has ended (F-6-3) or as a child-rearing parent (F-6-2) and are considering Korean nationality. The general requirements for simplified naturalization during an ongoing marriage are covered only briefly.

## Key Summary

- Period for simplified naturalization during the marriage: domicile in Korea for 2 years or more while married, or domicile in Korea for 1 year or more while married after 3 years have passed since the marriage
- Marriage breakdown route: the marriage could not continue because of the spouse's death, disappearance or another reason not attributable to the applicant; the remaining period is completed; the Minister of Justice finds it appropriate
- Child-rearing route: the applicant is raising, or must raise, a minor child born of the marriage; the period is completed; the Minister of Justice finds it appropriate
- In both routes the 5-year residence requirement and the permanent-residence status requirement are waived, but adulthood, good conduct, ability to support oneself, basic knowledge and national security requirements are still reviewed
- The period in Korea is counted as continuous lawful stay after lawful entry and completion of alien registration
- Marriage breakdown: add the Korean spouse's family relation certificates and removed family register, plus proof of the reason; child-rearing: add a birth certificate or similar and proof of raising the child
- The government fee for a naturalization application is 300,000 won per person (revenue stamp), and simplified naturalization applicants do not need letters of recommendation
- Those naturalized on marriage breakdown or child-rearing grounds are not on the list of people who may pledge not to exercise foreign nationality, so in principle they must renounce foreign nationality within 1 year

## 1. Is simplified naturalization possible even after the marriage has ended?

Simplified naturalization lets a person obtain naturalization permission without meeting the general naturalization requirements of 5 years or more of domicile in Korea and a status of stay allowing permanent residence. A foreigner whose spouse is a Korean national can apply after 2 years or more of continuous domicile in Korea while married, or after 1 year or more of continuous domicile in Korea while married once 3 years have passed since the marriage.

The Nationality Act separately covers cases where the marriage ended before this period was completed, and cases where the applicant did not meet the marriage requirement but is raising a child.

| Route | Who | Period requirement | Common |
|---|---|---|---|
| Marriage breakdown | A person who, while married and domiciled in Korea, could not continue a normal married life because of the spouse's death, disappearance or a reason not attributable to them | Complete the remaining part of the 2 years (or 3 years of marriage and 1 year of domicile) | The Minister of Justice finds it appropriate |
| Child-rearing | A person raising, or who must raise, a minor child born of the marriage with that spouse | Complete the 2 years (or 3 years of marriage and 1 year of domicile) | The Minister of Justice finds it appropriate |

## 2. What is required after the spouse's death or disappearance, or a divorce that was not your fault?

There are three key points. First, the reason must have arisen while the applicant was married and domiciled in Korea. Second, the reason must be the spouse's death or disappearance, or another reason for which the applicant is not responsible. Third, the applicant must complete the remaining part of the 2-year period (or 3 years of marriage and 1 year of domicile in Korea), and the Minister of Justice must find naturalization appropriate.

How the remaining period is counted, and how the time stayed after the marriage ended is reflected, needs to be confirmed case by case. In a divorce, it is important to show in documents that the main responsibility for the end of the marriage lies with the Korean spouse.

## 3. Which requirements apply if you are raising a child?

A person who is raising, or must raise, a minor child born of the marriage with the Korean spouse can obtain simplified naturalization even without meeting the marriage requirement of the ongoing-marriage route, if the period (2 years, or 3 years of marriage and 1 year of domicile in Korea) has been completed and the Minister of Justice finds it appropriate.

The child must be a minor born of that marriage. A child from a previous marriage, or a child who has already become an adult, does not meet this requirement.

## 4. How is the period of domicile in Korea counted?

The period for simplified naturalization is the time a foreigner has stayed lawfully and continuously in Korea after entering lawfully and completing alien registration. In the following cases, the stay is treated as continuous and the periods before and after are added together.

- Leaving Korea with a re-entry permit before the period of stay ends and re-entering within the permit period
- Leaving temporarily because the period of stay could not be extended, and re-entering within 1 month with an entry visa
- Similar cases where the Minister of Justice finds it appropriate to add the periods together

After the marriage ends, getting extensions as a person whose marriage has ended (F-6-3) or as a child-rearing parent (F-6-2) on time keeps the stay from being interrupted.

## 5. Are other requirements reviewed besides the period?

Simplified naturalization waives only the 5-year residence and permanent-residence status requirements. The following are still reviewed.

- Being an adult under the Civil Act of Korea
- Meeting the good-conduct requirement, such as complying with the law
- Being able to support oneself through one's own assets or skills, or by relying on family members who share a livelihood (simplified naturalization applicants submit one of the following, among others: proof of financial assets of 30 million won or more, documents for real estate ownership or a lease deposit of 30 million won or more, or a certificate of employment or of prospective employment)
- Having basic knowledge as a Korean national, such as Korean language ability and understanding of Korean customs
- The Minister of Justice finding that granting naturalization does not harm national security, public order or public welfare

Basic knowledge is checked through a comprehensive assessment and an interview. The applicant must sit the comprehensive assessment within 1 year from the application date, and naturalization is not granted with a score below 60 or an unsuitable result in the interview. A person who has completed the Social Integration Program, or who scored 60 or more in the comprehensive assessment within the last 3 years from the application date, may be exempted from the comprehensive assessment.

## 6. Which documents are submitted?

Submit the following with the naturalization application form to the Immigration Office (or its branch). Applicants aged 15 or older must apply in person.

| Category | Documents |
|---|---|
| Common | Proof of foreign status, proof of ability to support oneself, proof of relationship if a child is applying together |
| Marriage breakdown | The Korean spouse's family relation certificates and removed family register, and proof that the applicant could not continue a normal married life because of the spouse's death, disappearance or another reason not attributable to the applicant |
| Child-rearing | The Korean spouse's family relation certificates and removed family register, a birth certificate or similar showing there is a minor child born to the applicant and that spouse, and proof that the applicant is raising, or must raise, that child |

Letters of recommendation are not required for simplified naturalization applicants. The alien registration certificate and the record of entry and exit are checked through the immigration information system (they must be attached if the applicant does not consent to this check). The application fee is 300,000 won per person, paid with a government revenue stamp (children applying together are excluded).

Proof of a divorce that was not your fault is also required in the review for a person whose marriage has ended (F-6-3). Examples given in that stay review include the Korean spouse's missing-person (runaway) report, a hospital diagnosis for injuries from the spouse's violence, a prosecutor's decision not to indict, a confirmation from an accredited women's organization, a confirmation from a relative of the Korean spouse within the fourth degree, and a confirmation from the neighborhood head where the couple lived when the marriage broke down. Keeping the documents submitted for stay extensions well organized also helps when preparing a naturalization application.

## 7. What happens to your stay and your foreign nationality during the review?

Your period of stay must be managed separately while the naturalization review is under way. The basic approach is to extend your stay as a person whose marriage has ended (F-6-3), or as a child-rearing parent (F-6-2) if you are raising a child. A foreigner going through a nationality procedure such as naturalization can also apply for a change to Visiting and Joining Family status by submitting a letter of guarantee and a certificate of having applied for naturalization, among other documents.

A person who acquires Korean nationality by naturalization must renounce foreign nationality within 1 year. The law lists the people who may instead pledge not to exercise foreign nationality in Korea, and among marriage migrants only those naturalized through the ongoing-marriage route (2 years of domicile, or 3 years of marriage and 1 year of domicile) are on that list. A person naturalized on marriage breakdown or child-rearing grounds must in principle renounce foreign nationality, and loses Korean nationality if neither renunciation nor a pledge is made within 1 year. Exceptions for people who cannot renounce because of the law and system of their home country are set separately and need to be confirmed case by case.

Common problems include:

- Missing an extension after the marriage ended, so the continuous stay was interrupted
- Having the reason for divorce stated only in an agreement, with no objective evidence of the Korean spouse's fault
- Assuming a child from a previous marriage, or an adult child, meets the child-rearing requirement
- Missing the deadline to sit the comprehensive assessment (1 year from the application date)
- Assuming the deadline to renounce foreign nationality after naturalization can be met with a pledge

## Frequently Asked Questions

### I divorced my Korean spouse. Can I still be naturalized?

If, while married and domiciled in Korea, you could no longer continue married life for a reason not attributable to you, you have completed the remaining part of the required period, and the Minister of Justice finds it appropriate, you can obtain simplified naturalization.

### If my spouse died, do I still have to complete the full period?

Even if you did not complete the ongoing-marriage period (2 years, or 3 years of marriage and 1 year of domicile in Korea), you qualify for simplified naturalization if you complete the remaining period after your spouse's death and the Minister of Justice finds it appropriate.

### Which extra documents are needed if I am raising a child?

Submit the Korean spouse's family relation certificates and removed family register, a birth certificate or similar showing there is a minor child born to you and that spouse, and proof that you are raising, or must raise, that child.

### How do I show I can support myself?

Simplified naturalization applicants submit one of the following, among others: proof of financial assets of 30 million won or more, documents for real estate ownership or a lease deposit of 30 million won or more, or a certificate of employment or of prospective employment.

### Can I keep my home-country nationality after naturalization?

People naturalized on marriage breakdown or child-rearing grounds are not on the list of those who may pledge not to exercise foreign nationality, so in principle they must renounce foreign nationality within 1 year of acquiring Korean nationality. Cases where renunciation is difficult under home-country law need to be confirmed case by case.
