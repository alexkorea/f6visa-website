# Proof of Residence Documents for Marriage Migrants — What to Submit for Extensions, Foreigner Registration and Change of Address, and the Accommodation Confirmation

When preparing a Marriage Migrant (F-6) extension, "proof of residence documents" appear on almost every document list. These are documents that show where you actually live, and the examples given include not only a lease contract but also an accommodation confirmation, a notice of upcoming expiry of the period of stay received by post, utility bill payment receipts and dormitory fee receipts. This guide is for those who live in a home in their spouse's or parents-in-law's name and have no lease in their own name, and for those wondering what to submit after moving. The Korean spouse's housing requirement (visa screening) is a separate topic, so see the related guide below.

## Key Summary

- Examples of proof of residence: lease contract, accommodation confirmation, notice of upcoming expiry of the period of stay received by post, utility bill payment receipts, dormitory fee receipts, etc.
- Marriage migrant procedures: included in the documents for F-6-1, F-6-2 and F-6-3 extensions, F-6-2 foreigner registration, and change to or extension of Household Affairs (F-1-6), among others
- After moving: report the change of address within 15 days of moving in, attaching a lease contract, a sales contract or a document confirming the move
- No contract in your own name: the list of examples includes the accommodation confirmation, and which documents are accepted needs to be checked case by case
- Submitting forged or altered documents or documents containing false facts is prohibited, and a permit obtained by false or other improper means may be cancelled or changed

## 1. What are proof of residence documents?

Proof of residence documents are documents used to confirm where the applicant actually stays in Korea. The document list for marriage migrant extensions gives the following examples:
- Lease contract
- Accommodation confirmation
- Notice of upcoming expiry of the period of stay received by post
- Utility bill payment receipts
- Dormitory fee receipts

The list ends with "etc.", so it is not limited to these five. However, which documents and how many are enough may differ depending on the applicant's situation and the competent office's judgment, so this needs to be checked case by case.

## 2. In which procedures do marriage migrants submit them?

The procedures related to Marriage Migrant (F-6) whose document lists include proof of residence are:

| Procedure | Proof of residence |
|---|---|
| First extension for Spouse of a Korean National (F-6-1) (extension and foreigner registration within 90 days of entry) | Required |
| Extension for Spouse of a Korean National (F-6-1) | Required |
| Extension during separation, divorce litigation or a missing spouse (F-6-1) | Required (common documents) |
| First and later extensions for Child Rearing (F-6-2) | Required |
| Foreigner registration for those who entered on a Child Rearing (F-6-2) visa | A document that can prove the place of stay |
| First and later extensions for Marriage Breakdown (F-6-3) after a spouse's death, disappearance or divorce | Required |
| Change to and extension of Household Affairs (F-1-6) | Required |

You may also be asked for other documents deemed necessary for the review, so it is a good idea to check the competent immigration office's guidance again before filing.

## 3. What do I submit after moving?

A registered foreigner who changes their place of stay must report the move (change of address report) within 15 days of moving in to the city, county or district or town or neighbourhood office of the new place of stay, or to the immigration office with jurisdiction over it. When reporting, you submit your alien registration card, and it is returned after the change of address is written on it. One of the following is attached to the report:
- Lease contract
- Sales contract
- Another document confirming the move to the new place of stay, as determined by the Minister of Justice

If you put off the report after moving, the address on the proof of residence for your next extension may not match your registered place of stay, so report within the 15-day deadline first.

## 4. What if the home is not in my name?

Many marriage migrants live in a home in the name of their Korean spouse or parents-in-law, so it is common to have no lease in their own name. Besides the lease contract, the examples of proof of residence include the accommodation confirmation, the notice of upcoming expiry of the period of stay received by post and utility bill payment receipts, so if you have no contract in your own name, consider which of these can show that you actually live there. For reference, the documents for a Spouse of a Korean National (F-6-1) extension also include a resident registration certificate along with the marriage relation certificate (detailed).

Which documents fit your living arrangement and whether an accommodation confirmation alone is enough need to be checked case by case.

## 5. What does the accommodation provider prepare?

Many people wonder what the accommodation provider needs to prepare when an accommodation confirmation is submitted. However, in marriage migrant stay procedures, the form of the accommodation confirmation and the range of identity or housing documents the provider must submit with it are not specifically set out in the laws and regulations. What the provider needs to prepare therefore needs to be checked case by case, and it is safest to check with the competent immigration office before filing and then coordinate the schedule with the provider.

## 6. What happens if I submit documents that do not match the facts?

The Immigration Act prohibits, in connection with applications for permits such as extension of stay or change of status, applying by improper means such as submitting forged or altered documents as supporting evidence or submitting applications containing false facts, as well as arranging or encouraging such acts. If it comes to light that a permit was obtained by false or other improper means, the permit may be cancelled or changed. Writing false facts in applications or reports may also make you subject to an administrative fine. Do not borrow documents for a place where you do not actually live.

## 7. Common problems

- Living in a home in a spouse's or parents-in-law's name and going to file without preparing proof of residence
- Not reporting the change of address within 15 days of moving, so the addresses on documents do not match
- Delaying an extension application because the schedule with the accommodation provider does not work out
- Submitting documents with an address different from the actual place of residence
