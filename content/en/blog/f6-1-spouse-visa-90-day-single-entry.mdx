# Why Is the F-6-1 Spouse Visa Issued as a 90-Day Single-Entry Visa? Plans to Stay a Year, Multiple Entry for US Nationals, and the Short-Term Multiple-Entry Visa

The Spouse of a Korean National (F-6-1) visa issued by a Korean diplomatic mission is a single-entry visa with a stay of 90 days or less. This is because visas for spouses of Korean nationals are delegated to the authority of the head of the mission only within this range; US nationals receive a multiple-entry visa. If you plan to live together for a year or more, you complete alien registration and extension of stay at the competent immigration office within 90 days of entry.

## Key Summary

- Visa: single entry with a stay of 90 days or less (multiple entry for US nationals)
- The authority delegated to the head of the mission is limited to single-entry visas of 90 days or less
- To stay longer: alien registration and extension of stay at the immigration office (office or branch) for your address within 90 days of entry
- The first extension is in principle granted for 1 year from the date of entry
- The short-term (C-3-1) multiple-entry visa for foreign-national spouses of Koreans excludes applications made for marriage cohabitation
- The applicant’s passport must have at least 6 months of remaining validity

## 1. I plan to live in Korea for a year. Why do I get a 90-day visa?

For spouses of Korean nationals, only single-entry visas with a stay of 90 days or less are delegated to the authority of the head of the mission. So even if you apply saying you will stay for a year, the mission issues a marriage visa with a stay of 90 days or less. To stay beyond the period written on the visa, you obtain an extension of stay permit at the competent immigration office (office or branch) after entering Korea.

## 2. Why is it different for US nationals?

The Spouse of a Korean National (F-6-1) visa is issued as a single-entry visa, but US nationals are to receive a multiple-entry visa. The guidance on alien registration and extension of stay within 90 days of entry appears in the same item.

## 3. What do I do after entering?

Within 90 days of entering on the Marriage Migrant (F-6-1, 90 days) visa, apply for extension of stay and alien registration at the immigration office (office or branch) for your address. The stay granted is in principle 1 year from the date of entry. For nationals of countries covered by the international marriage guidance program, how the period is granted depends on whether the early adaptation program has been completed, so see the first-extension guide under Related Guides for the documents.

## 4. Can’t I just come and go on a short-term multiple-entry visa?

A foreign-national spouse of a Korean national is eligible for a short-term general (C-3-1) multiple-entry visa with a stay of 90 days and validity of 5 years. However, applications by a spouse of a Korean national for marriage cohabitation are excluded. In addition, holders of short-term visas in principle cannot change to Marriage Migrant (F-6) status inside Korea and must leave and obtain a visa from a mission abroad, so if you intend to live together, prepare for the F-6-1 visa from the start.

## 5. What should I watch for with the passport and documents?

- The applicant’s (foreign spouse’s) original passport must have at least 6 months of remaining validity
- The visa application form is available from the embassy and the HiKorea website
- The invitation letter, marriage relation certificate and similar documents must be within 3 months of preparation or issuance
- Where a review such as a direct interview with the applicant is needed, applications by a proxy may be restricted

## 6. Common problems

- Taking the visa’s 90 days as the whole permitted stay and missing the alien registration deadline
- Planning to live together after marriage but entering on a short-term multiple-entry visa and trying to change status in Korea
- The passport having less than 6 months of remaining validity, so a new passport must be issued first
- Preparing only a proxy application when the case requires an interview
