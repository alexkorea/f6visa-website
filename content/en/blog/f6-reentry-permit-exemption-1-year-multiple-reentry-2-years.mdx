# F-6 Marriage Migrants: 1-Year Re-entry Permit Exemption and the Multiple Re-entry Permit for Returns Within 2 Years

A person registered under marriage migrant status (F-6, including the former F-2-1 and F-2-10) who re-enters within 1 year of leaving is exempt from a re-entry permit, and someone returning after 1 year but within 2 years is guided to a multiple re-entry permit with a 2-year permit period. If the stay expires sooner, it is limited to the remaining stay period, and entry-restricted persons must obtain a re-entry permit with headquarters approval.

## Key Summary

- Return within 1 year of leaving: re-entry permit exempt (exemption period 1 year)
- Return after 1 year and within 2 years: multiple re-entry permit with a 2-year period
- If the stay expires in less than 1 or 2 years: limited to the stay period
- Entry-restricted persons need a re-entry permit with headquarters approval

## 1. What is the re-entry permit exemption?

A registered marriage migrant who re-enters within 1 year of the departure date can return without a separate re-entry permit. Covered statuses include marriage migrant (F-6) and the former F-2-1 and F-2-10.

## 2. What are the exemption period and exceptions?

The exemption period is 1 year. If the stay expires in less than 1 year, the exemption applies only within the stay period.

## 3. What if I stay away longer than 1 year?

A person re-entering after 1 year and within 2 years of departure is covered by the multiple re-entry permit, with a permit period of 2 years. If the stay expires in less than 2 years, the permit period is limited to the stay period.

## 4. What about entry-restricted persons?

Entry-restricted persons must obtain a re-entry permit with headquarters approval.

## 5. Checks before departure

- Alien registration is completed
- The stay expiry date is after the time you plan to return
- If you might exceed 1 year, confirm before leaving whether a multiple re-entry permit is needed

## 6. Common problems

- Assuming the “1-year exemption” applies when the stay is about to end
- Trying to return without a permit after exceeding 1 year
- Not knowing about an entry restriction and failing to check before leaving
