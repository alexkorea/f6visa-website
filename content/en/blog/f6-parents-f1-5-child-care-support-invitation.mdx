# Bringing Your Parents to Korea to Help With Childcare — Requirements and Stay Period for Parents of Marriage Migrants (F-1-5)

If you are raising a child in Korea on Marriage Migrant (F-6) status, your parents from your home country can be invited under the status "parents and other family of marriage migrants (F-1-5)" to help with childcare. The invitation is made by the Korean spouse or another eligible inviter, it can be filed during pregnancy or until the end of September of the year the child turns 9, and after entry your parents register as foreigners and extend their stay up to a point set by the child's age.

## Key Summary

- Visa: Visiting and Joining Family (F-1-5), single-entry visa of 90 days or less
- Who invites: the Korean spouse, a marriage migrant who has acquired Korean nationality or Permanent Residence (F-5), or a marriage migrant in a single-parent family
- Who is invited: the marriage migrant's father or mother (in exceptional cases, such as when neither parent can enter, a sibling or a child born of a prior marriage)
- Timing: during pregnancy or until the end of September of the year the child turns 9 (single-parent and multi-child families: the year the child turns 12)
- Number of invitations: up to 2 per child; 1 person at a time (parents may be invited together or one after the other)
- Stay: first extension within 1 year from entry, then up to 1 year at a time within 3 years from entry, until the end of March of the year the last child turns 10 (single-parent and multi-child families: 13)

## 1. Who can invite?

The following can invite:
- The Korean spouse
- A marriage migrant who has acquired Korean nationality or Permanent Residence (F-5)
- A marriage migrant in a single-parent family

Here, a "marriage migrant" means a foreign national residing in Korea who is or was married to a Korean national. For a family living as Marriage Migrant (F-6) in an ongoing marriage with a Korean spouse, the Korean spouse is the eligible inviter on this list. A "single-parent family" is a family made up of a marriage migrant who alone is raising a child born of a marriage with a Korean national, or a Korean national who alone is raising a child born of the relationship with a marriage migrant who died during the marriage, together with that child. Which category fits your family needs to be checked case by case.

## 2. Can I invite family members other than my parents?

In principle, the person invited is the marriage migrant's father or mother. Only when neither of the marriage migrant's parents can enter Korea and the invited person has no minor children of their own can a sibling or a child born of a prior marriage be invited. In that case you additionally submit documents confirming that both parents cannot enter because of death, illness, old age of 60 or over or a similar reason, and family relationship documents showing the invited person's direct family.

## 3. Until when, and how many times, can I invite?

- Timing: as of the application date, the marriage migrant (or their spouse) is pregnant, or it is before the end of September of the year the child turns 9.
- Single-parent and multi-child families: until the end of September of the year the child turns 12. A "multi-child family" has 3 or more children who are all minors under the Civil Act, and the timing and extension periods are judged by the age of the last child.
- Number of invitations: up to 2 per child. When the father and mother are invited together or one after the other, the count applies to each separately, so inviting both counts as 2.
- Number of people: 1 in principle, with parents only allowed together or one after the other. If a father or mother who entered is still staying in Korea, no other home-country family member can be invited under this status.

## 4. Are there cases where the invitation is restricted?

Yes. If a violation of Korean law is found for the inviter or for a home-country family member who previously entered on that inviter's invitation, invitations for this visa are restricted for a certain period. Examples include a previously invited family member who is currently staying illegally, or who in the past received a departure order or deportation order for illegal stay, illegal employment or a criminal offence. Visa issuance is also restricted for a certain period if the invited person has violated Korean law or has received a departure order or deportation order. The length of the restriction differs by case and needs to be checked individually.

## 5. Which documents are needed?

- Applicant (parent): visa application form, passport, 1 standard photo, fee
- Inviter: copy of ID, letter of invitation, letter of guarantee (guarantee period of 3 years from entry), pledge to prevent illegal stay and employment
- Korean family documents: the inviter's basic certificate, family relation certificate, marriage relation certificate, resident registration (copy), and the child's family relation certificate (if pregnant, a pregnancy diagnosis or maternal health record book). If there are 2 or more children, submit the family relation certificates of all children.
- Home-country family documents: official family relationship documents issued by that country's government showing all of the marriage migrant's direct family and siblings
- If the child is of school age: certificate of enrollment

## 6. How long can they stay after entry?

Your parents enter on a single-entry visa of 90 days or less, then complete foreigner registration and the first extension of stay. They submit the integrated application form, passport, proof of residence, a pledge not to work, a standard photo, the fee and the Korean family documents; the proof of residence can be replaced by the resident registration (copy) of the inviter or of the child receiving childcare support.
- First extension: within 1 year from entry, until the end of March of the year the child (if there are 2 children, the last child) turns 10
- Later extensions: within 3 years from entry, up to 1 year at a time until the same point. If the child is of school age, a certificate of enrollment is also submitted.
- Single-parent and multi-child families: the point becomes the end of March of the year the child turns 13.
- If the child is of school age but the inviter does not fulfil the duty to send the child to school, extensions are restricted.

Extensions must be applied for while staying in Korea, and leaving Korea after applying may lead to refusal.

## 7. Common problems

- Preparing on the assumption that the F-6 holder is the inviter, then having to recheck who is eligible to invite
- Missing the end of September of the year the child turns 9
- Trying to invite a sibling as well while one parent is already in Korea
- Home-country family documents that do not show all direct family and siblings
- The child reaching school age without a certificate of enrollment being prepared, causing problems at the extension

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Does the F-6 holder personally invite the parents?

The eligible inviters are the Korean spouse, a marriage migrant who has acquired nationality or Permanent Residence (F-5), and a marriage migrant in a single-parent family. For an F-6 family living with a Korean spouse, the Korean spouse is the eligible inviter.

### Until what age of the child can I invite?

As of the application date, during pregnancy or until the end of September of the year the child turns 9. For single-parent and multi-child families, until the end of September of the year the child turns 12.

### Can both my father and mother come?

In principle 1 person is invited, but parents may be invited together or one after the other. In that case the count applies to each parent, so it counts as 2.

### How long can my parents stay in Korea?

The first extension is within 1 year from entry, then up to 1 year at a time within 3 years from entry, until the end of March of the year the last child turns 10 (13 for single-parent and multi-child families).

### If my parents cannot come, can I invite a sibling?

Only when neither of the marriage migrant's parents can enter and the invited person has no minor children can a sibling or a child born of a prior marriage be invited, with documents confirming why the parents cannot enter.
