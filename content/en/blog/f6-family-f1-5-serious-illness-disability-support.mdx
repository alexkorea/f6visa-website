# A Family Member With a Serious Illness or Severe Disability? Inviting Home-Country Parents on Humanitarian Grounds (F-1-5)

If someone in a Marriage Migrant (F-6) family has a serious illness or severe disability, you can consider the "humanitarian circumstances" type of the status "parents and other family of marriage migrants (F-1-5)", which lets the family invite the marriage migrant's parents from the home country to help. You apply with the same basic documents as the childcare support type plus proof of the serious illness or disability, and after entry the stay can be extended for as long as the humanitarian circumstances continue.

## Key Summary

- Visa: Visiting and Joining Family (F-1-5), single-entry visa of 90 days or less
- Humanitarian grounds: the marriage migrant, the Korean spouse or a child has a serious illness or severe disability
- Who is invited: the marriage migrant's father or mother (in exceptional cases, such as when neither parent can enter, a sibling or a child born of a prior marriage)
- Number of people: 1, with parents only allowed together or one after the other
- Additional documents: medical bill receipts or similar showing a serious (rare and intractable) illness or the special co-payment status, a disability certificate or similar
- Stay: first extension within 1 year from entry, then up to 1 year at a time within 3 years from entry, for as long as the humanitarian circumstances continue

## 1. How does the humanitarian type differ from the childcare support type?

The F-1-5 status has a childcare support type and a humanitarian circumstances type. Under the childcare support type, the invitation must be made during pregnancy or by the end of September of the year the child turns 9, and the stay ends at a point based on the child's age, such as the end of March of the year the child turns 10. The humanitarian type exists to support marriage migrant families with a serious illness or severe disability, so extensions after entry are based on how long the humanitarian circumstances continue. Whether a separate timing rule or limit on the number of invitations applies to the humanitarian type needs to be checked case by case.

## 2. Who can invite?

The eligible inviters for the humanitarian type are:
- The Korean spouse or a marriage migrant who has acquired Korean nationality or Permanent Residence (F-5)
- A marriage migrant in a single-parent family
- (Humanitarian grounds) where the marriage migrant, the Korean spouse or a child has a "serious illness" or "severe disability"

A "marriage migrant" means a foreign national residing in Korea who is or was married to a Korean national. Exactly how these items must be met together differs from family to family and needs to be checked case by case.

## 3. Whom can I invite, and how many people?

- In principle: the marriage migrant's father or mother
- Exception: if neither of the marriage migrant's parents can enter Korea and the invited person has no minor children, a sibling or a child born of a prior marriage. In that case you additionally submit documents confirming that both parents cannot enter because of death, illness, old age of 60 or over or a similar reason, and family relationship documents showing the invited person's direct family.
- Number of people: 1 in principle, with parents only allowed together or one after the other. If a father or mother who entered through the procedure is still staying in Korea, no other home-country family member can be invited under this status.

If a violation of Korean law is found for the inviter or for a family member who previously entered on that inviter's invitation, or if the invited person has received a departure order or deportation order, invitations or visa issuance are restricted for a certain period.

## 4. How is a serious illness or severe disability proven?

- Serious illness: medical bill receipts or similar on which a "serious illness (rare and intractable disease)" or the "special co-payment status" is stated
- Severe disability: a disability certificate or similar. If the "overall degree of disability" field of the disability certificate states "severe disability" or "severe degree of disability", it is recognized as a severe disability.

Whether other documents such as a medical certificate are also needed may vary during screening. The head of the overseas mission may add or omit some attachments where needed to examine the purpose of entry, the genuineness of the invitation and the eligibility of the inviter and the invited person.

## 5. Which documents are submitted with the visa application?

The basic documents for the humanitarian type are the same as for the childcare support type, plus proof of the serious illness or disability.
- Applicant (parent): visa application form, passport, 1 standard photo, fee
- Inviter: copy of ID, letter of invitation, letter of guarantee (guarantee period of 3 years from entry), pledge to prevent illegal stay and employment
- Korean family documents: the inviter's basic certificate, family relation certificate, marriage relation certificate, resident registration (copy) and the child's family relation certificate (if pregnant, a pregnancy diagnosis or maternal health record book); if there are 2 or more children, the certificates of all children
- Home-country family documents: official family relationship documents issued by that country's government showing all of the marriage migrant's direct family and siblings
- Proof of the serious illness or disability

## 6. How long can they stay after entry?

Your parents enter on a single-entry visa of 90 days or less, then complete foreigner registration and the first extension of stay.
- Registration and first extension documents: integrated application form, passport, proof of residence, a pledge not to work, standard photo, fee, Korean family documents and proof of the serious illness or disability. The proof of residence can be replaced by the resident registration (copy) of the inviter or of the child.
- First extension: within 1 year from entry, for as long as the humanitarian circumstances continue
- Later extensions: submit the integrated application form, passport, proof of residence, standard photo, fee and the family, illness and disability documents; up to 1 year at a time within 3 years from entry, for as long as the humanitarian circumstances continue

The immigration office may add or omit some documents where needed for screening. Extensions must be applied for while staying in Korea, and leaving Korea after applying may lead to refusal.

## 7. Common problems

- Medical bill receipts that do not state a serious (rare and intractable) illness or the special co-payment status
- Submitting a disability certificate without checking what the "overall degree of disability" field says
- Trying to invite another home-country family member while one parent is already in Korea
- Not preparing up-to-date documents showing the humanitarian circumstances are continuing at the time of extension
- Applying the child-age rules of the childcare support type to the humanitarian type as they are

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What counts as humanitarian circumstances?

The humanitarian grounds are that the marriage migrant, the Korean spouse or a child has a "serious illness" or "severe disability". Whether a specific case qualifies needs to be checked individually.

### Which document proves a severe disability?

If the "overall degree of disability" field of the disability certificate states "severe disability" or "severe degree of disability", it is recognized as a severe disability.

### How is a serious illness proven?

With medical bill receipts or similar on which a "serious illness (rare and intractable disease)" or the "special co-payment status" is stated.

### How long can my parents stay?

The first extension is within 1 year from entry, then up to 1 year at a time within 3 years from entry, for as long as the humanitarian circumstances continue.

### Can I invite both my father and mother?

In principle 1 person is invited, but parents may be invited together or one after the other.
