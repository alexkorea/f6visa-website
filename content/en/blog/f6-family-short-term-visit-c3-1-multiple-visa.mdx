# Want Your Parents and Children From Home to Visit Often? The 5-Year Multiple-Entry Short-Term Visit Visa (C-3-1) for Marriage Migrant Families and Extensions in Korea

If you have maintained a normal marriage with a Korean spouse for 1 year or more on Marriage Migrant (F-6) status, your parents and minor children in your home country fall under the "special rule for families of marriage migrants and persons who acquired Korean nationality" for a short-term (C-3-1) multiple-entry visa with a 90-day stay and 5-year validity. It suits short, repeated visits, and extending the stay inside Korea requires meeting separate extension conditions.

## Key Summary

- Special rule: families of marriage migrants and persons who acquired Korean nationality (C-3-1, multiple-entry visa)
- Eligible: parents and minor children of a person who married a Korean national, registered the marriage in both countries and has maintained a normal marriage for 1 year or more
- Visa: Short-Term General (C-3-1), 90-day stay, 5-year multiple-entry visa
- Documents: visa application form, copy of passport, documents proving the family relationship
- Extension in Korea: possible within a 90-day period of stay from entry if there is no suspicion of illegal work and the need is recognized
- For long stays to help with childcare there is a separate status, parents and other family of marriage migrants (F-1-5)

## 1. Who can get this multiple-entry visa?

Under the multiple-entry visa rules, the "special rule for families of marriage migrants and persons who acquired Korean nationality (C-3-1)" covers:
- Parents and minor children of a person who married a Korean national, registered the marriage in both countries and has maintained a normal marriage for 1 year or more
- Parents and children of a person who acquired Korean nationality (whether or not a marriage migrant)
- The spouse of a Korean national who holds foreign nationality (excluding a spouse of a Korean national applying for the purpose of married cohabitation)

For a Marriage Migrant (F-6) family, check the first item. The marriage must be registered both in Korea and in your home country, and a normal marriage must have continued for 1 year or more. Because the item covers minor children, a child who has become an adult cannot use it.

## 2. What does the visa provide?

It is a Short-Term General (C-3-1) multiple-entry visa with a 90-day period of stay and 5-year validity. Within the validity period the holder can enter several times, and each stay may last 90 days. The maximum period of stay that can be granted at one time under Short-Term Visit (C-3) is also 90 days.

## 3. Which documents are needed?

- Visa application form
- Copy of passport
- Documents proving the family relationship

Which specific documents prove the family relationship, and whether translation or confirmation is needed, may differ by overseas mission, so check in advance. For an application for parents, it helps to prepare documents that show both the relationship between the marriage migrant and the parents and the marriage with the Korean spouse.

## 4. Can the stay be extended after entry?

If the activity falls within Short-Term Visit (C-3), there is no suspicion of illegal work and the need for an extension is recognized, an extension is possible within a 90-day period of stay from the date of entry. Examples of extension reasons are:
- A person who entered to visit relatives or similar with no suspicion of illegal work (such as family of a marriage migrant)
- Proof of an unavoidable reason, such as no ship or flight to leave on, or an accident or illness not foreseeable at entry
- A multiple-entry visa holder explaining the need to stay beyond the period granted at entry

For the extension you submit the application form, original passport, fee and documents explaining the need for the extension. Extensions must be applied for while in Korea, and leaving after applying may lead to refusal. How long an extension is actually granted depends on the reason and screening and needs to be checked individually.

## 5. For a long stay to help with a child, which fits better: C-3-1 or F-1-5?

The purpose and length differ.
- Short-term (C-3-1) multiple-entry visa: 90-day stay, 5-year validity. Suited to repeated short visits.
- Parents and other family of marriage migrants (F-1-5): a single-entry visa of 90 days or less for entry to support childcare and similar purposes; after entry, foreigner registration and extensions allow a longer stay. However, there are timing conditions, such as during pregnancy or until the end of September of the year the child turns 9.

Which suits your family depends on the length and purpose of the stay and the child's age, so it needs to be checked case by case.

## 6. Common problems

- Registering the marriage only in Korea and not in the home country, so the "registered in both countries" condition is not met
- Applying before the marriage reaches 1 year
- Assuming an adult child can use the same special rule
- Believing a single entry allows a stay of more than 90 days
- Trying to cover a long stay, such as for childcare, with only a short-term visa

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Does every parent of a marriage migrant get a 5-year multiple-entry visa?

The special rule covers parents and minor children of a person who married a Korean national, registered the marriage in both countries and has maintained a normal marriage for 1 year or more. Actual issuance is decided through screening.

### How long can they stay per entry?

The period of stay is 90 days. They can enter several times during the 5-year validity.

### Can the stay be extended in Korea?

If there is no suspicion of illegal work and the need is recognized, an extension is possible within a 90-day period of stay from entry. A family visit by family of a marriage migrant is one of the example reasons.

### Can an adult child use this rule?

The marriage migrant family item of this rule covers parents and minor children. For an adult child, other options need to be checked individually.

### Which status suits a long stay to look after a child?

For childcare support, consider parents and other family of marriage migrants (F-1-5). After entry, registration and extensions allow a longer stay, but there are timing conditions for the invitation.
