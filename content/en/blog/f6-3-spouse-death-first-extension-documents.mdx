# When the Korean Spouse Dies: How Does the First Extension as a Marriage-Terminated Person (F-6-3) Work?

If you were staying as the spouse of a Korean national (F-6-1) in a normal marriage in Korea and your Korean spouse died of illness, accident or another cause, you fall under the stay extension for a marriage-terminated person (F-6-3). The first extension needs the application form, passport and alien registration card, fee, proof of death (death certificate, basic certificate showing the death, etc.), family relation documents, a foreigner occupation report and proof of address.

## Key Summary

- Eligible: F-6-1 holders in a normal marriage whose Korean spouse died of illness, accident, etc.
- Proof of death: death certificate, basic certificate stating the death, etc.
- Family proof: detailed marriage certificate; child’s family relation certificate if there is a child
- Occupation report and proof of address are also submitted

## 1. What happens to my stay if my spouse dies?

The manual provides an F-6-3 status for marriage-terminated persons (death, missing, divorce). For someone staying as F-6-1, it is handled as the “first stay extension.”

## 2. Who is covered?

A person staying as the spouse of a Korean national (F-6-1) who was in a normal marriage with the national in Korea, and whose Korean spouse died of illness, accident or another cause.

## 3. Which documents are required?

- Application form (Form No. 34), passport and alien registration card, fee
- Proof of the spouse’s death (death certificate, basic certificate stating the death, etc.)
- Family relation documents (detailed marriage certificate, etc.)
- Child’s family relation certificate if a child was born to the couple
- Foreigner occupation report
- Proof of address (lease, accommodation confirmation, expiry notice mail, utility receipt, dormitory fee receipt, etc.)
- Other documents the office considers necessary

## 4. What about later extensions?

A foreigner staying in marriage-terminated (F-6-3) status after the first extension follows a separate extension procedure, submitting the application, passport, registration card, fee, the child’s family relation certificate if any, the occupation report and proof of address.

## 5. Points to watch

- Obtain recent certificates reflecting the death
- Organize material showing the marriage was ongoing (residence records, etc.)
- Schedule filing before the stay expires

## 6. Common problems

- Death and marriage certificates that do not match in content
- Missing occupation report or address proof leading to supplement requests
- Filing close to the expiry date

### Must I leave Korea immediately if my spouse dies?

The manual has a procedure for extending stay as a marriage-terminated person (F-6-3). Individual circumstances should be confirmed with the immigration office.

### Which proof of death is needed?

A death certificate and a basic certificate stating the spouse’s death, among others.

### Are there extra documents if we have a child?

Yes, the child’s family relation certificate if a child was born to the couple.

Yes, the manual lists the foreigner occupation report among the documents.

A separate extension procedure for those staying in F-6-3 status is described in the manual.
