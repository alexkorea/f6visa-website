# Raising Your Foreign-National Child From a Previous Marriage in Korea — Change of Status and Extension for Children of Marriage Migrants From a Prior Marriage (F-1-52)

If you are married to a Korean national on Marriage Migrant (F-6) status and your foreign-national minor child from a previous marriage is with you in Korea, the child can stay under the Visiting and Joining Family status "child of a marriage migrant born of a prior marriage (F-1-52)". It covers biological children who have not been adopted by the Korean spouse; the grant is 2 years if the child attends a school recognized for public education and 1 year if not, and it cannot exceed the marriage migrant's own period of stay.

## Key Summary

- Who: a minor child staying in Korea who is the biological child of a marriage migrant (F-6, F-5-2) in an ongoing marriage with a Korean national, born of a prior marriage and not adopted by the Korean spouse
- Grant period: 2 years if enrolled at a school recognized for public education, 1 year if not
- Cap: cannot be granted beyond the marriage migrant's period of stay
- Adult rule: if the child entered high school before turning 19 and continues at the same school, up to 1 year at a time until the end of February of the graduation year (age cap 21)
- Key documents: proof of custody, letters of guarantee from the marriage migrant and the Korean spouse, the child's official birth documents

## 1. Which children qualify for F-1-52?

A child who meets all of the following:
- The child of a marriage migrant (F-6 or F-5-2) whose marriage with a Korean national continues
- A biological child born of a prior marriage
- Not adopted by the Korean spouse
- A minor staying in Korea

If the Korean spouse has adopted the child, this status does not apply, and the stay rules that apply then need to be checked case by case. Also, since the child must be staying in Korea to apply for a change of status, how a child living abroad would enter Korea needs to be checked separately.

## 2. How long is the grant period?

- Enrolled at a school recognized for public education: 2 years
- Not enrolled at such a school: 1 year

The same rule applies to extensions for minors (under 19). However, the child's period cannot be granted beyond the marriage migrant's period of stay, so if the parent's period ends sooner, the child's grant is fitted within it. It is a good idea to manage the parent's F-6 extension schedule and the child's extension schedule together.

## 3. Which documents are submitted for the change of status?

1. Passport, integrated application form, 1 standard photo, fee, alien registration card (if any)
2. The Korean spouse's basic certificate, family relation certificate, marriage relation certificate (detailed version) and resident registration copy
3. The marriage migrant's passport, ID (of the relevant country) and alien registration card
4. The minor foreign child's passport and the original and a copy of the official birth document issued by the relevant country (for example a Chinese household register or birth certificate)
5. Proof of custody of the minor foreign child born of the prior marriage (such as a divorce judgment)
6. Letters of guarantee from the marriage migrant who has custody and is raising the child in Korea and from the Korean spouse
7. Certificate of enrollment (if enrolled at a school recognized for public education)
8. Proof of residence (such as a lease), and a tuberculosis diagnosis for nationals of high-risk tuberculosis countries

Documents issued abroad must carry an apostille from the issuing government or consular confirmation by the Korean mission in that country. Documents issued in Korea, such as the resident registration copy, should be issued within 3 months unless a separate validity period applies.

## 4. Which documents are needed for an extension?

The extension documents are the same for minors and adults.
- Integrated application form, passport, alien registration card, fee
- The Korean spouse's basic certificate, family relation certificate, marriage relation certificate (detailed version) and resident registration copy
- Certificate of enrollment (if enrolled at a school recognized for public education)
- Proof of residence (such as a lease), and a tuberculosis diagnosis for nationals of high-risk tuberculosis countries (if required)

Extensions must be applied for while staying in Korea, and leaving Korea after applying may lead to refusal.

## 5. Can the child no longer stay after turning 19?

There is a rule allowing the child to finish high school after becoming an adult. If a child of a marriage migrant born of a prior marriage who is lawfully staying under F-1-52 or Residence (F-2-2) is 19 or older, but entered a high school recognized for public education before turning 19, is still enrolled and wishes to continue at the same school, the stay can be extended by up to 1 year at a time until the end of February of the high-school graduation year. The age cap for this stay is 21. How a school transfer or new enrollment after turning 19 is treated needs to be checked case by case.

## 6. Does a school-age child need to report anything separately?

Yes. Registered foreigners aged 6 to 18 must report their school enrollment. When first entering elementary, middle or high school, or when enrollment status changes (including moving up to the next school level), report within 15 days, and when applying for any stay permit, write the school name and similar details in the "school enrollment" field of the integrated application form. Since enrollment also determines the F-1-52 grant period (2 years or 1 year), keep it accurately up to date.

## 7. Common problems

- Preparing for F-1-52 even after the Korean spouse has adopted the child
- No document showing custody, such as a divorce judgment, or no translation or confirmation of it
- A short remaining stay for the parent, resulting in a short grant for the child
- Missing the 15-day enrollment report after moving up a level or changing schools
- High-school entry timing just before age 19 not matching, so the adult rule cannot be used

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Does a child adopted by the Korean spouse qualify for F-1-52?

No. F-1-52 covers biological children born of a prior marriage who have not been adopted by the Korean spouse. The rules after adoption need to be checked individually.

### How long is the grant?

2 years if enrolled at a school recognized for public education, 1 year if not, and never beyond the marriage migrant's period of stay.

### Is a custody document really required?

For the change of status, you submit proof of custody of the minor foreign child born of the prior marriage (such as a divorce judgment).

### What happens when the child turns 19?

If the child entered high school before turning 19 and continues at the same school, the stay can be extended by up to 1 year at a time until the end of February of the graduation year, with an age cap of 21.

### Do we have to report a school change?

Registered foreigners aged 6 to 18 must report within 15 days of first entering school or a change in enrollment status (including moving up a level).
