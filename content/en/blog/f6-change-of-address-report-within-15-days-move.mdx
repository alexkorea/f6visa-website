# Moved House as a Marriage Migrant? Report Your Change of Address Within 15 Days — Where to Report, Documents and the Link to Move-in Registration

When a marriage migrant who has completed foreigner registration moves house, they must make a move-in report, that is, a report of change of place of stay, within 15 days from the date of moving in, to the head of the city, county or district (si, gun, gu) or town, township or neighbourhood (eup, myeon, dong) of the new place of stay, or to the head of the immigration office with jurisdiction over the new place of stay. When reporting you hand in your alien registration card, and the office writes the new place of stay on the card and returns it. This report takes the place of move-in registration under the resident registration system.

This guide is for people staying in Korea with foreigner registration on Marriage Migrant (F-6) status who are about to move or have just moved.

## Key Summary

- Who: a registered foreigner (including marriage migrants) who changes their place of stay
- Deadline: within 15 days from the date of moving in
- Where: the head of the si, gun, gu or eup, myeon, dong of the new place of stay, or the head of the immigration office with jurisdiction over it (also possible through the information network designated by the Minister of Justice)
- Documents: change of place of stay report form, alien registration card, documents confirming the move such as a lease contract or sales contract
- Result: the change is written on the alien registration card and the card is returned (for a mobile alien registration card, recording it on the mobile card can take its place)
- Effect: the report of change of place of stay takes the place of resident move-in registration
- Not reporting: breaching the reporting duty is subject to penalties set out in the Immigration Act

## 1. Who must report a change of place of stay?

Under the Immigration Act, a foreigner who has completed foreigner registration must report when they change their place of stay. This includes people registered on Marriage Migrant (F-6) status, and any change of place of stay is subject to reporting.

If a person under 17 must report a change of place of stay but does not do so themselves, their parents or other persons prescribed by Presidential Decree must report. If a registered foreign child in a marriage migrant family moves with you, remember the child's report as well.

## 2. What is the deadline?

Within 15 days from the date of moving in. The reference point is the day you finish moving and enter the new place of stay. The contract date and the actual move-in date can differ, so it is best to count the deadline from the day you actually moved and not delay.

For reference, when foreigner registration details change, such as name, sex, date of birth and nationality, or passport number, issue date and expiry date, you must also report the change of registration details to the immigration office with jurisdiction over your place of stay within 15 days. This is a separate report from the report of change of place of stay.

## 3. Where do I report?

You can report to either of the following:

- The head of the si, gun, gu or eup, myeon, dong of the new place of stay (community service centre, etc.)
- The head of the immigration office with jurisdiction over the new place of stay

The move-in report can also be made through the information network designated by the Minister of Justice. The office that receives the report is required to notify the immigration office with jurisdiction over the previous place of stay. If you report to the immigration office, that office notifies the head of the si, gun, gu or eup, myeon, dong of the new place of stay, and the si, gun, gu or eup, myeon, dong requests the transfer of the foreigner registration record from the previous place of stay.

## 4. Which documents do I prepare?

- Change of place of stay report form (the prescribed form differs depending on the office you report to)
- Alien registration card
- Documents confirming that you moved: a lease contract, a sales contract, or other documents designated by the Minister of Justice

When you have no contract in your own name, for example when moving into a home in your spouse's name, which documents are accepted as "other documents designated by the Minister of Justice" may be explained differently by each office, so this needs to be checked case by case.

## 5. What happens to my alien registration card?

The office that receives the report writes the change of place of stay on your alien registration card and returns it. At this point the change is written and a confirmation stamp for the change of place of stay report is applied before the card is handed back. If a person issued a mobile alien registration card reports through the electronic civil service window, recording the change on the mobile alien registration card can take the place of writing it on the card.

## 6. How does this relate to move-in registration at the community service centre?

Foreigner registration and the report of change of place of stay under the Immigration Act take the place of resident registration and move-in registration. In other words, a foreign marriage migrant completes move-in registration through the report of change of place of stay. Also, where a resident registration card or a copy or abstract of the resident register is needed in legal procedures or transactions, the alien registration card (including the mobile alien registration card) or a certificate of foreigner registration takes its place. This is why the report of change of place of stay must be done first for your new address to appear correctly on various documents after moving.

## 7. What happens if I do not report?

A person who breaches the duty to report a change of place of stay is subject to penalties set out in the Immigration Act. In addition, immigration officers and others may investigate the facts if there is considerable reason to suspect that the reported details differ from the facts.

The extension of stay documents for marriage migrants include proof of place of stay (lease contract, confirmation of accommodation provided, notice of upcoming expiry of stay sent by post, utility bill receipts, dormitory fee receipts, etc.). Proof of place of stay shows where you actually live, so if you have moved it is safest to complete the report within the deadline.

Common problems include:
- Counting the 15 days from the contract rather than the actual moving date
- Only the Korean spouse makes a move-in report and the foreign spouse's report of change of place of stay is left out
- Missing the report for a foreign child living with you
- Getting a new passport and thinking the report of change of registration details is the same as the report of change of place of stay

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Within how many days of moving must I report?

Within 15 days from the date of moving in, to the head of the si, gun, gu or eup, myeon, dong of the new place of stay, or to the head of the immigration office with jurisdiction.

### Can I report at the community service centre?

Yes. You can report to the head of the si, gun, gu or eup, myeon, dong of the new place of stay, or to the immigration office. It can also be done through the information network designated by the Minister of Justice.

### Do I have to make a change of place of stay report and a move-in report separately?

The report of change of place of stay under the Immigration Act takes the place of resident move-in registration.

### What should I bring?

The change of place of stay report form, your alien registration card, and documents confirming the move, such as a lease contract or sales contract.

### What if I report late?

Breaching the duty to report a change of place of stay is subject to penalties set out in the Immigration Act. If you have moved, it is safest to report within the deadline.
