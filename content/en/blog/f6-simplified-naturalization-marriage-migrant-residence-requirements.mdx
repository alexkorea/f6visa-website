# Simplified Naturalization for Marriage Migrants in Korea — Can You Apply After 2 Years of Married Residence, or 1 Year After 3 Years of Marriage?

Yes, you can. The Nationality Act provides that a foreigner whose spouse is a Korean national may receive naturalization permission without meeting the 5-year residence requirement and the permanent-residence status requirement of general naturalization, if they have had a continuous domicile in Korea for 2 years or more while married to that spouse, or if 3 years have passed since the marriage and they have had a continuous domicile in Korea for 1 year or more while married. The other requirements, such as legal adulthood, good conduct, ability to support oneself and basic knowledge, are still reviewed.

This guide is for people staying in Korea on a marriage migrant (F-6) visa while living with a Korean spouse who are thinking about naturalization instead of, or after, permanent residency.

## Key Summary

- Simplified naturalization while married: ① continuous domicile in Korea for 2 years or more while married, or ② 3 years since the marriage + continuous domicile in Korea for 1 year or more while married
- Exempt from the 5-year residence and permanent-residence status requirements of general naturalization; adulthood, good conduct, ability to support oneself, basic knowledge and national security requirements still apply
- The residence period is the period of lawful continuous stay after lawful entry and completed alien registration; a departure with a re-entry permit and return within its period keeps the count continuous
- Livelihood documents: proof of financial assets of 30 million won or more, real estate or a lease deposit of 30 million won or more, or a certificate of employment or of expected employment (for the applicant or family members sharing a livelihood)
- Simplified naturalization applicants are not required to submit letters of recommendation and submit a family relations certificate of the Korean spouse
- Comprehensive assessment (below 60 out of 100 means refusal) and interview; those who completed the Social Integration Program are eligible for exemption from the comprehensive assessment
- Application fee for naturalization permission: 300,000 won per person (excluding accompanying children); nationality is acquired when you take the oath and receive the naturalization certificate
- After naturalizing while married, you must renounce your foreign nationality or pledge not to exercise it within 1 year

## 1. How does simplified naturalization differ from general naturalization?

General naturalization requires a continuous domicile in Korea for 5 years or more and a status of stay that allows permanent residence in Korea. A foreigner married to a Korean can receive naturalization permission without these two requirements in either of the two cases below. In other words, you can apply while staying on a marriage migrant (F-6) visa, without first obtaining permanent residence (F-5).

| Case | Length of marriage | Required continuous domicile in Korea while married |
|---|---|---|
| First case | Not specified | 2 years or more |
| Second case | 3 years since the marriage | 1 year or more |

For example, if a couple married abroad, lived together there and came to Korea after 3 years of marriage, they may consider applying under the second case after 1 year of continuous residence in Korea. Separate simplified naturalization requirements apply when the marriage ended because of the spouse's death or disappearance or for reasons not attributable to you, or when you are raising a minor child born of the marriage; these are covered in a separate guide.

## 2. How are the 2-year and 1-year periods counted?

The residence period is the period during which the foreigner stayed lawfully and continuously in Korea after lawful entry and completion of alien registration. In the following cases the stay is treated as continuous, and the periods before and after the departure are added together.

- You obtained a re-entry permit before your period of stay ended, departed, and re-entered within the permit period
- You left temporarily because, for example, your stay could not be extended, and re-entered within 1 month with an entry visa
- The Minister of Justice finds it appropriate to add the periods for reasons equivalent to the above

The 2 years or 1 year must also be a period of domicile "while married." Periods spent in Korea under another status before the marriage was registered are hard to include. The immigration office checks your record of alien registration and entry and departure records through its system (if you do not consent, you attach them yourself).

## 3. What other requirements are reviewed?

Simplified naturalization only waives the 5-year residence and permanent-residence status requirements. You must still meet the following.

| Requirement | Content |
|---|---|
| Adulthood | Legal adult under the Korean Civil Act |
| Good conduct | Requirements set by Ministry of Justice ordinance, such as obeying the law |
| Ability to support oneself | Ability to make a living by your own assets or skills, or by relying on family members sharing a livelihood |
| Basic knowledge | Basic knowledge as a Korean national, such as Korean language ability and understanding of Korean customs |
| Public interest | The Minister of Justice recognizes that granting naturalization does not harm national security, public order or public welfare |

For good conduct, the principle is that you should not fall under any of the following. However, the Minister of Justice may still recognize good conduct by considering how the violation happened, the degree of harm to the public interest, contribution to society, humanitarian circumstances and so on.

- Less than 10 years since the end of, or exemption from, the execution of a sentence of imprisonment without labor or heavier
- Less than 7 years since the end of the probation period of a suspended sentence of imprisonment without labor or heavier
- Less than 5 years since paying a fine imposed by a criminal sentence
- Less than 2 years since a suspension of sentence or a suspension of indictment
- Less than 10 years since departing under a deportation order, or less than 5 years since departing under a departure order
- Unpaid national taxes, customs duties or local taxes

## 4. Which documents show the ability to support oneself?

A simplified naturalization applicant proves that the applicant or family members sharing a livelihood can support themselves with one of the following.

- Proof of financial assets (deposits, savings, securities, etc.) of 30 million won or more
- Proof of owning real estate worth 30 million won or more by official price, actual transaction price or a commercial bank's published market price, or a copy of a lease contract with a deposit of 30 million won or more
- A certificate of employment or a certificate of expected employment
- Other documents the Minister of Justice recognizes as equivalent

The amounts are lower than for general naturalization, which requires income of at least the previous year's gross national income per capita or assets of 60 million won or more. Assets in the Korean spouse's name can be used if the spouse is a family member sharing a livelihood with you.

## 5. What are the documents and procedure?

You file the naturalization application form (Annex Form No. 2) together with documents proving the requirements at the immigration office. The basic attachments for simplified naturalization while married are:

- A document proving that you are a foreigner
- Proof of ability to support oneself (section 4 above)
- A family relations certificate of the Korean spouse (if you married abroad and the marriage is not recorded in the spouse's family relations register, a document proving the marriage instead)
- If a minor child will acquire nationality with you, a document proving the relationship
- Documents set by the Minister of Justice that are needed to create the family relations register

Simplified naturalization applicants do not need letters of recommendation written by two or more Korean nationals. The Korean spouse's resident registration abstract is checked by the office through shared administrative information. The fee is 300,000 won per person for a naturalization application (excluding accompanying children), paid with government revenue stamps.

After filing, identity checks, criminal record checks and a review of your stay are carried out, and you may be asked to appear and state your views or to submit materials, and your residence may be inspected on site. A foreign minor child can apply together by indicating accompanying acquisition on the application form; if the parents are divorced, you must prove in writing that you have parental authority or custody.

## 6. How do the comprehensive assessment and interview work?

A comprehensive assessment and an interview are held to review the naturalization requirements. You must take the comprehensive assessment within 1 year of the application date; if you do not take it, or score below 60 out of 100, naturalization is not permitted. An "unsuitable" result in the interview also leads to refusal. The interview checks Korean language ability, your attitude as a Korean national and your belief in the liberal democratic basic order.

| Who | Exemption |
|---|---|
| Completed the Social Integration Program | Eligible for exemption from the comprehensive assessment |
| Scored 60 or more in the comprehensive assessment within the last 3 years from the application date | Eligible for exemption from the comprehensive assessment |
| Aged 60 or older | Eligible for exemption from the comprehensive assessment |
| Completed the Social Integration Program and scored 60 or more in the comprehensive assessment | Eligible for exemption from the interview |

Once permission is granted, you acquire Korean nationality when you take the oath of nationality and receive the naturalization certificate at the designated date and place. If you cannot attend because of illness, an accident or the like, you must submit a statement of reasons for absence by the day before the designated date, and a new date is designated within 1 month.

## 7. What happens to your original nationality after naturalization?

A foreigner who has acquired Korean nationality and holds a foreign nationality must renounce the foreign nationality within 1 year of the date of acquisition. A person naturalized while married (2-year residence or 3 years of marriage + 1-year residence) may instead pledge not to exercise the foreign nationality in Korea.

- Renouncing the foreign nationality: submit a certificate of renunciation (loss) of nationality issued by that country's consul or similar to the Minister of Justice → a confirmation of renunciation is issued
- Pledge not to exercise foreign nationality: submit the pledge to the immigration office while domiciled in Korea → a pledge confirmation is issued
- If you do neither within 1 year, you lose Korean nationality when the period ends

The acquisition of nationality is notified to the family relations registration office of your registration base and published in the Official Gazette. Whether your original country allows multiple nationality depends on that country's law, so check it before making the pledge.

## 8. Common problems

- Counting periods before the marriage was registered, or before alien registration, toward the 2 years
- Leaving without a re-entry permit and returning on a different visa, which breaks the residence period
- Applying less than 5 years after paying a criminal fine, or less than 2 years after a suspension of indictment
- Applying without knowing about unpaid local or national taxes
- Not taking the comprehensive assessment within 1 year of applying
- Losing Korean nationality again by not renouncing the foreign nationality or making the pledge within 1 year of naturalization

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can I apply for simplified naturalization without permanent residency (F-5)?

Yes. Simplified naturalization while married does not require the permanent-residence status of general naturalization, so you can apply while holding a marriage migrant (F-6) visa.

### Three years have passed since our marriage, but I have been in Korea less than 1 year. Can I apply?

Even in the 3-year marriage case you need a continuous domicile in Korea for 1 year or more while married, so you should apply after completing 1 year.

### Can I show the ability to support myself with my Korean spouse's assets?

The documents must prove that you or family members sharing a livelihood can support themselves, so a spouse living with you can provide financial assets or real estate of 30 million won or more, a certificate of employment and so on.

### Does completing the Social Integration Program exempt me from the test?

Those who completed the Social Integration Program are eligible for exemption from the comprehensive assessment. For the interview, completers who scored 60 or more in the comprehensive assessment are eligible for exemption.

### Do I have to give up my original nationality?

A person naturalized while married must, within 1 year, either renounce the foreign nationality or pledge not to exercise it in Korea. If you do neither, you lose Korean nationality.
