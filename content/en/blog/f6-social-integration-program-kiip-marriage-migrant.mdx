# Marriage Migrants and the Social Integration Program (KIIP) — What You Learn and How Completion Is Used in Permanent Residency (F-5-2) and Residence (F-2) Reviews

The Social Integration Program (KIIP) is a program of education, information and counselling that the Minister of Justice runs to support the social adaptation of foreigners seeking to acquire Korean nationality, permanent residency and the like. For people staying as Marriage Migrants (F-6), it is used to show the basic competency requirement when later changing to permanent residency (F-5-2), or when a child-rearing parent (F-6-2) who has raised a child to adulthood changes to Residence (F-2). This guide is for marriage migrants who want to know when, and with which documents, the program is used.

## Key Summary

- Nature: a program of education, information and counselling run by the Minister of Justice to support the social adaptation of foreigners seeking to acquire nationality, permanent residency and the like
- Content: Korean language education, education on understanding Korean society, job skills development support, education on school life for foreign parents of minors, and more
- Assessments: a preliminary assessment, stage-by-stage assessments and a comprehensive assessment that decides completion
- Benefits: completers can be given preferential treatment in visa issuance and stay-related permissions as provided by law
- Permanent residency (F-5-2): the basic competency requirement is proven by completing the program or scoring 60 or more (out of 100) on the comprehensive assessment
- Change from child-rearing parent (F-6-2) to Residence (F-2): basic competency is assessed as education hours at stage 4 or above of the program

## 1. What kind of program is it?

The Immigration Act provides that the Minister of Justice may run a social integration program of education, information, counselling and the like to support the social adaptation of foreigners seeking to acquire Korean nationality, permanent residency and the like. The Minister can designate institutions, corporations or organizations with the necessary specialists and facilities as program operators, so the actual classes take place at designated operators.

Applying to participate and managing assessments can be done online through an information network that the Minister of Justice builds and operates. Which operator and schedule are available to you, and which course you are placed in under your current status, need to be checked case by case.

## 2. What do you learn and how are you assessed?

The social integration program consists of:
- Korean language education
- Education on understanding Korean society
- Job skills development support education
- Education on understanding school life and the like for foreigners who are guardians of minors
- Other education, information, counselling and the like that the Minister of Justice considers necessary for foreigners' social adaptation

Participants can be given a preliminary assessment, stage-by-stage assessments to measure learning outcomes, and a comprehensive assessment that decides completion. Entrusted operators may charge education costs or assessment fees within amounts announced by the Minister of Justice, so check the costs before applying.

## 3. What benefits come with completion?

The Immigration Act provides that the Minister of Justice may give preferential treatment to program completers in visa issuance, stay-related permissions and the like, as provided by that Act or related laws. In other words, completion alone does not decide a permission; it is used where the requirements of each status set out how completion is reflected. For marriage migrants, the main places are the permanent residency (F-5-2) and Residence (F-2) reviews below.

## 4. How is it used in the permanent residency (F-5-2) review?

To acquire permanent residency you must have the basic competency needed to keep living in Korea, such as Korean language ability and an understanding of Korean society and culture. This basic competency is recognized if you have completed the social integration program or scored 60 or more out of 100 on the comprehensive assessment.

When a spouse of a Korean national who has stayed in Korea continuously for 2 years or more as a Marriage Migrant (F-6) applies for marriage migrant permanent residency (F-5-2), the basic competency documents are:
- If you completed the program: a social integration program completion certificate (Korean Immigration Permanent Residency Aptitude Course or Korean Immigration Naturalization Aptitude Course)
- If you scored 60 or more on the comprehensive assessment: a KIPRAT pass certificate (Korean Immigration Permanent Residency Aptitude Test) or a KINAT pass certificate (Korean Immigration Naturalization Aptitude Test)

In some cases the basic competency requirement can be eased or waived under a Ministry of Justice notice, and whether it applies to you needs to be checked case by case. The other permanent residency requirements, such as conduct and financial self-sufficiency, are covered in a separate guide under Related Guides.

## 5. How is it used when a child-rearing parent (F-6-2) changes to Residence (F-2)?

A foreign mother or father who, after the marriage with a Korean national ended, raised the child born to them and the Korean spouse in Korea until the child became an adult can apply to change to Residence (F-2). The basic settlement requirements reviewed are good conduct, financial self-sufficiency (40% of the standard median income) and basic competency (education hours at stage 4 or above of the social integration program).

The basic competency document is the social integration program education confirmation, and the following are also accepted:
- Social integration program Korean Language and Culture Test pass certificate (KLCT)
- Social integration program preliminary assessment score report (only 81 points or more)
- Social integration program completion certificate (Naturalization Aptitude Course, Permanent Residency Aptitude Course), KINAT pass certificate, KIPRAT pass certificate

When extending the period of stay after changing to Residence (F-2) in this way, the same basic settlement requirements apply, and the education confirmation is not needed again if already submitted.

## 6. How is it different from the communication requirement for the marriage visa?

The list of communication requirement documents for applying for a spouse of a Korean national (F-6-1) visa abroad includes a Korean Education Center or King Sejong Institute completion certificate, a TOPIK score report and a completion certificate from a designated Korean language institution, and a social integration program completion certificate is not separately listed there. Think of the social integration program as something mainly completed while staying in Korea and later used to show basic competency in permanent residency or Residence reviews. For the communication requirement at the visa stage, see the separate guide under Related Guides.

## 7. Common problems

- Submitting only the education confirmation for permanent residency (F-5-2) without a completion certificate or comprehensive assessment pass certificate
- Believing that completing the program alone will get permanent residency approved without the other requirements
- Submitting a preliminary assessment score report under 81 points for the Residence (F-2) change
- Treating the communication requirement for the marriage visa and the basic competency requirement in Korea as the same thing
- Delaying the application without checking operator schedules and costs

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Who runs the social integration program?

The Minister of Justice runs it and can designate institutions, corporations or organizations with the necessary specialists and facilities as operators.

### Does completing it get me permanent residency straight away?

No. Completion is a way to prove the basic competency requirement for F-5-2, and you must also meet other requirements such as 2 or more years of continuous stay as a Marriage Migrant (F-6), good conduct and financial self-sufficiency.

### Which documents prove basic competency for F-5-2?

A social integration program completion certificate (Permanent Residency Aptitude Course or Naturalization Aptitude Course), or a KIPRAT or KINAT pass certificate showing 60 or more on the comprehensive assessment.

### Can a child-rearing parent changing to F-2 use a preliminary assessment score?

A preliminary assessment score report is accepted only if it shows 81 points or more. The basic document is the social integration program education confirmation.

### Is the communication requirement for the marriage visa proven with the social integration program?

The list of communication requirement documents for the visa application does not separately include a social integration program completion certificate. Check that requirement against its own list, such as Korean Education Center or King Sejong Institute certificates and TOPIK score reports.
