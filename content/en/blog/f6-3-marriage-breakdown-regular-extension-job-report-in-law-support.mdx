# From the Second Extension on F-6-3 (Marriage Breakdown) — Regular Extension Documents and the Special Rule for Supporting Your Former Spouse's Parents

After receiving the first extension as a person whose marriage has broken down (F-6-3) because of the Korean spouse's death or disappearance, or a divorce for which you were not responsible, you apply for an extension of stay again to keep staying under the same status. These second and later extensions use a different set of documents from the first one. Instead of documents proving the death, disappearance or divorce, the core documents are the application form, passport, alien registration card, a family relation certificate in the child's name if you have children, a foreigner occupation report and proof of place of stay.

This guide is for people already staying on F-6-3 who are preparing their next extension, and for people who bear responsibility for their divorce but are supporting the Korean spouse's parents or family. The first extension itself is covered separately in the articles listed under Related Guides.

## Key Summary

- Who: a foreigner staying on Marriage Breakdown (F-6-3) status after the first extension
- Documents: application form (Form No. 34), passport, alien registration card, fee; (if there is a child born to the couple) a family relation certificate in the child's name; foreigner occupation report; proof of place of stay; other documents deemed necessary for review
- Difference from the first extension: documents proving the death, disappearance or divorce and fault are not on the list
- Period: the maximum period of stay that can be granted at one time for Marriage Migrant (F-6) status is 3 years; the actual period is decided through review
- Special rule: even if the divorce was due to the foreigner's fault, if the foreigner supports the Korean spouse's parents or family, stay on F-6-3 is permitted within 1 year after the facts are confirmed and support documents are submitted

## 1. How are the first extension and later extensions different?

Extensions of stay for persons whose marriage has broken down (F-6-3) fall into two broad groups. The first is the initial extension for someone who was staying as the spouse of a Korean national (F-6-1) and whose spouse died, went missing, or who divorced for reasons they were not responsible for. The second is the extension for a foreigner who, after that first extension, is staying on Marriage Breakdown (F-6-3) status.

In the first case, the key is proving why the marriage broke down, so documents such as a death certificate, a court ruling declaring disappearance, a divorce judgment and proof of fault are included. In the second case, you are already staying on F-6-3, so the documents focus on confirming your current situation (children, occupation, place of stay).

## 2. Who is covered by this extension?

It covers a foreigner staying on Marriage Breakdown (F-6-3) status. F-6-3 is the sub-status for a person who, while staying in Korea married to a Korean national spouse, cannot maintain a normal marital relationship because of the spouse's death or disappearance or other reasons for which they are not responsible.

For reference, a person whose marriage to a Korean national has broken down but who does not qualify as F-6-3, and who must stay in Korea for reasons such as division of property or settling household affairs, has a separate route: stay permission for Household Affairs (F-1-6), granted within 6 months each time.

## 3. Which documents are submitted?

The documents for extending the stay of a foreigner staying on F-6-3 after the first extension are:

1. Application form (Form No. 34), passport, alien registration card, fee
2. (If there is a child born to the couple) a family relation certificate in the child's name
3. Foreigner occupation report
4. Proof of place of stay (lease contract, confirmation of accommodation provided, notice of upcoming expiry of stay sent by post, utility bill receipts, dormitory fee receipts, etc.)
5. Other documents deemed necessary for review

The foreigner occupation report states the work you are currently doing. Marriage Migrant (F-6) status is not restricted in employment by status category, so working while on F-6-3 is possible, and at extension you report your occupation through this form. Proof of place of stay shows where you actually live, so if you have moved it is best to complete the change of address report first.

## 4. Do I have to submit the documents from the first extension again?

The document list for second and later extensions does not include proof of the spouse's death, proof of disappearance, a marriage relation certificate (detailed) showing the divorce, divorce litigation documents or proof of fault. However, the list ends with "other documents deemed necessary for review", so if the office requests additional material you must submit it. What may be requested needs to be checked case by case.

The family relation certificate in the child's name is submitted, as at the first extension, when there is a child born to the couple. It does not apply if there are no children.

## 5. How long is granted at one time?

For Marriage Migrant (F-6) status, the maximum period of stay that can be granted at one time is 3 years. However, the item for second and later F-6-3 extensions does not state a separate permitted period, so how many years are actually granted is decided through review and needs to be checked case by case. It is safest to prepare the documents in advance as the expiry date approaches.

## 6. The special rule for staying even if the divorce was your fault

The first extension for a divorce without fault on your part carries one special rule. Even if the divorce was due to the foreigner's fault, if that foreigner supports the Korean spouse's parents or family, stay on F-6-3 is permitted within 1 year after the facts are confirmed and documents related to the support are collected.

- Situation: the main responsibility for the divorce lies with the foreigner
- Condition: supporting the Korean spouse's parents or family
- Procedure: confirmation of the facts and submission of documents proving the support
- Permission: F-6-3 within 1 year

Which documents are accepted as proof of support is not specifically defined, so this needs to be checked case by case. It is important to organize the documents so that the fact of support is shown continuously. How the next extension is reviewed after permission under this rule is also not separately stated, so it needs to be checked case by case, together with whether the support continues.

## 7. Common problems

- Preparing only the same documents as the first time for the second extension and leaving out the foreigner occupation report or proof of place of stay
- Not reporting a change of address after moving, so the proof of place of stay does not match the registered address
- Not submitting the family relation certificate in the child's name when there is a child
- Giving up without checking the special rule because the divorce was your fault, even though you support your former spouse's parents
- Assuming the permitted period is the same every time and not checking the expiry date

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Do I need to submit the divorce judgment or death certificate again for the second F-6-3 extension?

They are not on the document list for second and later extensions. However, if documents deemed necessary for review are requested, you must submit them.

### Can I work while staying on F-6-3?

Marriage Migrant (F-6) status is not restricted in employment by status category. At extension you submit a foreigner occupation report.

### How many years can I get when extending?

The maximum period that can be granted at one time for Marriage Migrant (F-6) status is 3 years. The actual period for second and later extensions is decided through review and needs to be checked case by case.

### If the divorce was my fault, can I not get F-6-3?

The principle is a divorce for reasons you were not responsible for. However, if you support the Korean spouse's parents or family, there is a special rule under which stay on F-6-3 can be permitted within 1 year after the facts are confirmed and support documents are submitted.

### If I have no children, is the family relation certificate unnecessary?

The family relation certificate in the child's name is submitted when there is a child born to the couple.
