# If Your Marriage Migrant Stay Extension Is Not Granted — The Departure Deadline on the Notice, Deferral Requests and Next Options

If you are staying in Korea as a Marriage Migrant (F-6), applied to extend your period of stay and were not granted it, the first thing to check is the departure deadline written on the notice. When an extension is not granted, you are informed in a prescribed form, and that form states a departure deadline within a range not exceeding 14 days from the date of issue. This guide is for marriage migrants whose extension application in Korea was not granted, explaining what to check before the deadline and which procedures are available. A visa refused at a Korean embassy or consulate abroad follows a different procedure, so see the separate guide under Related Guides.

## Key Summary

- When to apply for an extension: before your period of stay ends, and while you are staying in Korea.
- Notice of refusal: you are informed in a prescribed form, which states a departure deadline within a range not exceeding 14 days from the date of issue (where necessary, the expiry date of the period of stay already granted can be the deadline).
- Deferral of the departure deadline: if it is clear you cannot leave by the deadline because there is no ship or aircraft or for an unavoidable reason such as illness, you can apply for a deferral.
- Extension of stay for departure: available where, for example, you cannot leave for an unavoidable reason; beyond 30 days it needs the approval of the Minister of Justice.
- If you overstay: staying beyond your period of stay without extension permission makes you subject to deportation, and there are penalty provisions as well.
- Next steps: to come back you may, in principle, need to leave and obtain a visa at an embassy or consulate abroad, so first check the reason for refusal.

## 1. By when must I apply for an extension?

The Immigration Act provides that a foreigner who wants to stay beyond their period of stay must obtain the Minister of Justice's permission to extend it before the period ends. You apply before your period of stay ends by submitting the extension application with the prescribed documents to the immigration office, which forwards it to the Minister of Justice with its opinion. For someone who has completed foreigner registration, the permitted period is written on the residence card when the extension is granted.

The maximum period that can be granted at one time for Marriage Migrant (F-6) status is 3 years; for a spouse of a Korean national (F-6-1), extensions are granted within 1 year, or within 3 years if you are raising a child born to you and your Korean spouse. Any application for stay permission must be made while you are staying in Korea, and leaving Korea after filing may lead to refusal. Because the decision depends on review, prepare your documents well before the expiry date.

## 2. How am I notified if it is refused?

When the Minister of Justice does not grant an extension, the applicant is informed in a form set by Ministry of Justice ordinance. That form must clearly state a departure deadline within a range not exceeding 14 days from the date of issue. Where the Minister of Justice considers it necessary, however, the expiry date of the period of stay already granted can be set as the departure deadline.

When a change of status is not granted, the person can be allowed to stay under the status already granted, and the deadline may then be left out, but that applies to change-of-status applications. If your extension application was not granted, check the departure deadline date on the notice first. The deadline differs from person to person, so count your remaining time from the date on the notice.

## 3. If you cannot leave by the deadline — deferral of the departure deadline

If a person who received a notice of refusal clearly cannot leave by the deadline because there is no ship or aircraft to leave on, or because of illness or another unavoidable reason, the departure deadline can be deferred.

- How to apply: submit a departure deadline deferral application with materials explaining the reason to the immigration office.
- Deferral period: where there is no ship or aircraft, until a period separately set by the Minister of Justice taking into account the expected number of departures and transport conditions; in other cases, until the reason ceases to exist.

A deferral is a procedure for explaining why you cannot leave; it is not a new review of the extension. The key is to prepare materials showing the reason, such as a medical certificate or flight circumstances, before the deadline.

## 4. What is an extension of stay for departure?

When a person's permitted period of stay is expiring, the period can be extended if the person has completed foreigner registration, finished the activities of their status and wants to stay briefly for purposes such as domestic travel, or if there is no ship or aircraft to leave on or the person cannot leave for another unavoidable reason. If the extension exceeds 30 days, the approval of the Minister of Justice is required. You apply by submitting the extension application with materials explaining the reason.

There is an important caution. A person granted an extension of stay for departure, in principle, cannot change to spouse of a Korean national (F-6-1) status inside Korea and must leave and enter with a visa issued by an embassy or consulate abroad; a person granted a departure deadline deferral is not eligible for a change of status either. Also, when the period of stay in Korea is counted for permanent residency review, time under an extension for departure or a departure deadline deferral is counted as time out of the country.

## 5. What happens if I miss the departure deadline?

Staying beyond your period of stay without extension permission violates the Immigration Act. Such a person is subject to deportation, and the same Act also contains penalty provisions for it. How a case is handled after the deadline has passed needs to be checked case by case, so the priority is to leave by the deadline or use a procedure such as a deferral or an extension for departure.

## 6. If you want to apply again or challenge the decision

- Check the reason for refusal: first find out from the notice and the immigration office what was lacking. Whether it can be remedied or you can apply again needs to be checked case by case.
- Applying again from abroad: to come back as a marriage migrant after leaving, you apply for a visa at an embassy or consulate abroad. Visa refusals and reapplication are covered in a separate guide under Related Guides.
- If remedy proceedings are under way: where a foreign spouse of a Korean national applies for an extension while court proceedings, a criminal investigation or other remedy proceedings on grounds of domestic violence are under way, the extension can be granted until those proceedings end. After that, it can also be granted where necessary for recovery from harm.
- Separation, divorce suit or missing spouse: a spouse of a Korean national (F-6-1) in these situations has separate extension criteria and documents.
- Challenging the decision: how and by when a refusal can be challenged depends on the notice and the case and needs to be checked case by case.

## 7. Common problems

- Trying to apply for an extension after the expiry date
- Leaving Korea after filing an extension and before the result, leading to refusal
- Assuming you can stay until the expiry date without checking the departure deadline on the notice
- Applying for a deferral without materials explaining the reason
- Believing you can change back to F-6-1 inside Korea after receiving an extension for departure

## Frequently Asked Questions

### If my extension is refused, can I stay until my period of stay expires?

The departure deadline on the notice is what counts. It is set within a range not exceeding 14 days from the date of issue, and where the Minister of Justice considers it necessary, the expiry date of the period already granted can be the deadline.

### Can I apply for an extension after my period of stay has ended?

The extension must be obtained before the period of stay ends, and the application must also be made before it ends.

### What if I am ill and cannot leave by the deadline?

If it is clear you cannot leave by the deadline for an unavoidable reason such as illness, you can apply with a departure deadline deferral application and materials explaining the reason. In such cases the deferral can last until the reason ceases to exist.

### After an extension for departure, can I change back to marriage migrant status inside Korea?

A person granted an extension of stay for departure, in principle, cannot change to F-6-1 status inside Korea and must leave and enter with a visa issued by an embassy or consulate abroad.

### Can I object to the refusal?

How and by when a refusal can be challenged depends on the notice and the case and needs to be checked case by case. Some procedures may have time limits, so check as soon as you receive the notice.
