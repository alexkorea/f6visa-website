# When the Child Becomes an Adult: Requirements and Timing for an F-6-2 Parent to Change to Resident (F-2)

A foreign parent who raised the child born to the Korean spouse in Korea until adulthood after the marriage ended can apply to change to Resident (F-2) if they are lawfully staying as F-6-2 on the application date and have stayed in Korea continuously for 5 years or more under F-6-1, F-6-2 and F-6-3. The application window runs from 4 months before the child becomes an adult under the Civil Act until the F-6-2 stay expiry date, and conduct, livelihood (40% of the standard median income) and basic literacy (Social Integration Program Level 4 or higher hours) are checked.

## Key Summary

- Eligible: F-6-2 parent who raised the child in Korea until adulthood
- Stay: 5 years or more continuously under F-6-1, F-6-2 and F-6-3, and a life base in Korea for both parent and child
- Timing: from 4 months before the child becomes an adult under the Civil Act until the F-6-2 expiry date
- Settlement requirements: good conduct, livelihood (40% of the median income), basic literacy (Social Integration Program Level 4 or higher)

## 1. What kind of change is this?

A procedure for a foreign parent who raised the child born to the Korean spouse in Korea until adulthood after the marriage ended to change to Resident (F-2) status.

## 2. What are the qualifications?

- Lawfully staying as child-rearing (F-6-2) on the application date
- Stayed in Korea continuously for 5 years or more as spouse of a national (F-6-1), child-rearing (F-6-2) or marriage-terminated (F-6-3)
- For both applicant and child, time spent abroad within 5 years before the application must not exceed time spent in Korea
- Living with the child is regarded as actually raising the child

## 3. When can I apply?

From 4 months before the child becomes an adult under the Civil Act until the expiry date of the F-6-2 stay.

## 4. How are income and basic literacy assessed?

The settlement requirements are good conduct, livelihood (40% of the standard median income) and basic literacy (Social Integration Program Level 4 or higher hours). If the applicant or the child has a “serious illness” or “serious disability,” the livelihood requirement is waived. The income amount changes yearly, so check the current notice.

## 5. Which documents are submitted?

- Passport, standard photo, fee, integrated application form, proof of address
- Child’s resident registration and proof of child-rearing expenses (if needed, credit card history, messages with the child, confirmations from others)
- Income certificate (add objective evidence such as pay records or sales and expense documents if insufficient; waiver cases add serious illness or disability documents)
- Basic literacy documents such as the Social Integration Program certificate (completion certificates and KINAT or KIPRAT pass certificates are also accepted)

## 6. Common problems

- Missing the window so the F-6-2 expiry date passes
- Long periods abroad within the 5 years failing the life-base requirement
- Delays in preparing basic literacy documents
