# Do Marriage Migrants Have to Carry a Passport or Residence Card at All Times? — The Duty to Carry and Show ID, the Mobile Residence Card, and What to Do If You Lose It

Marriage migrants (F-6) living in Korea are foreigners too, so under the Immigration Act they must always carry an identity document such as a passport or alien registration card (residence card), and must show it when an immigration officer or other authorized public official asks for it in connection with their duties. It does not have to be the passport, though: the residence card and the mobile residence card are also among the documents the law lists.

This guide is for marriage migrants who have completed alien registration and live in Korea, and for their Korean spouses.

## Key Summary

- Who: foreigners staying in Korea (those under 17 are exempt from the carry duty)
- What: one of the passport, seafarer's identity document, foreigner entry permit, residence card, mobile residence card or landing permit (the law calls these "passport, etc.")
- When to show it: when an immigration officer or other authorized public official asks for it in connection with their duties
- Mobile residence card: same legal effect as the residence card, issued to only 1 of your own phones
- Violations: breaking the duty to carry or show may lead to the penalty provisions of the Immigration Act
- If lost: submit a reissue application and 1 photo to the immigration office with jurisdiction over your place of stay

## 1. Who has to carry it?

The Immigration Act provides that "foreigners staying in the Republic of Korea" must always carry a passport, etc. Spouses staying with marriage migrant (F-6) status are included. Foreigners under 17, however, are exempt from the carry duty.

A residence card may not be issued to someone under 17; in that case a sticker with the alien registration number is attached to the passport. When a foreigner who did not receive a residence card turns 17, they must apply for a residence card at the immigration office with jurisdiction over their place of stay within 90 days. If a marriage migrant family has a child of foreign nationality, it is worth remembering this point.

## 2. What should you carry? Can the residence card replace the passport?

The documents listed in the law are the passport, seafarer's identity document, foreigner entry permit, residence card, mobile residence card and landing permit, joined by "or." In other words, carrying any one of them is enough, and a marriage migrant who has completed alien registration can carry the residence card or mobile residence card instead of the passport.

Before alien registration you do not yet have a residence card, so you must carry your passport. A foreigner who intends to stay more than 90 days from the date of entry must complete alien registration at the immigration office with jurisdiction over their place of stay within 90 days of entry. Whether carrying a copy of the passport or residence card satisfies the duty is not written in the law and needs to be confirmed case by case.

## 3. What is the mobile residence card?

The mobile residence card is a residence card installed in encrypted form on a mobile phone. It can be issued to a foreigner who has received a residence card, and it has the same legal effect as the residence card.

- Application: submit an application to the immigration office with jurisdiction over your place of stay and present your passport or residence card. People holding a residence card with an embedded integrated circuit (IC) chip can apply through their phone.
- Number of devices: it can be issued to only 1 of the phones you use.
- Validity: until the expiry date of your period of stay.
- Use: where the physical residence card, or the name, photo, alien registration number and other details on it, need to be checked, checking the mobile residence card can take its place.

## 4. When do you have to show it?

A foreigner who must carry a passport, etc. must show it when an immigration officer or other authorized public official asks for it in connection with their duties. Which officials may ask in which duties depends on the situation and needs to be confirmed case by case.

On the other hand, the law says that immigration officers and other authorized officials must carry and show a badge indicating their authority when carrying out duties such as inspecting homes or belongings, or asking questions or requesting materials. If you need to confirm that the person is an official, you can calmly ask them to identify themselves.

Also, where a resident registration card or resident registration copy or extract is needed in procedures and transactions under laws and regulations, a residence card (including the mobile residence card) or a certificate of alien registration is used in its place.

## 5. What happens if you do not carry or show it?

The Immigration Act contains penalty provisions for people who violate the duty to carry or show a passport, etc. What action is actually taken depends on the case and needs to be confirmed case by case. Making a habit of taking your residence card or mobile residence card when you leave home is the simplest precaution.

## 6. If you lose your residence card

If you lose your residence card, you can get it reissued at the immigration office with jurisdiction over your place of stay. Besides loss, reasons for reissue include the card being too worn to use, running out of space for entries, receiving a change of status permit, and filing certain changes to alien registration details.

- What to submit: a residence card reissue application with 1 photo attached.
- Reasons other than loss: when applying for a reason other than loss, such as wear or lack of space, the original residence card must be attached.
- Mobile residence card: it can be reissued when the residence card has been reissued or when you change phones. If you lose the phone on which the mobile residence card is installed, it may be invalidated.

While waiting for the reissue, carrying your passport is the practical way to keep the carry duty.

## 7. Common problems

- Leaving the passport at home before alien registration has been done
- Losing the residence card, putting off the reissue, and having no document at all when ID is needed
- Changing phones without having the mobile residence card reissued, so it cannot be checked
- Missing the 90-day window to apply for a residence card after a child of foreign nationality turns 17

## Frequently Asked Questions

### If I have a residence card, can I leave my passport at home?

The documents listed in the law include the residence card and the mobile residence card, so carrying one of them can replace the passport. When a foreigner leaves Korea, they must take a valid passport and go through departure inspection.

### Is the mobile residence card on my phone enough?

The mobile residence card has the same legal effect as the residence card and is also among the documents listed in the law. If your phone battery is dead you cannot show it, so it is good to prepare for that.

### Does my child have to carry a residence card too?

Foreigners under 17 are exempt from the carry duty. If no residence card was issued to a child under 17, an application must be made within 90 days of turning 17.

### Do I have to show it to anyone who asks?

The duty to show applies when an immigration officer or other authorized public official asks in connection with their duties. When officials carry out duties such as inspections or questioning, they are required to carry and show a badge indicating their authority.

### I lost my residence card. What should I do?

Submit a residence card reissue application with 1 photo to the immigration office with jurisdiction over your place of stay and get it reissued. In the meantime it is best to carry your passport.
